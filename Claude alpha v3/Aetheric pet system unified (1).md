# PART 1: FOUNDATION & WORLD CONTEXT

## The Core Premise

Creatures in the Ætherium Codex are not "pets" in the Pokémon sense—they are the **primary magical fauna and monsters** of the world. They emerge naturally from **Planar Bleed**: when rifts to other planes tear open, native creatures are exposed to alien magical laws, and the most adaptable survive through fundamental, irreversible transformation.

### Three Outcomes of Aetheric Saturation
1. **Empowerment** — Creatures gain new abilities, forms, and resilience
2. **Corruption** — Creatures degrade, mutate chaotically, become unstable
3. **Annihilation** — Creatures cease to exist; consumed by planar forces

Those that successfully adapt become the setting's magical fauna: Eidolons, Vespidans, Cephalarchs, and countless unnamed species shaped by the overlapping influence of multiple planes.

### The World as System

This is **not a game within the Codex**—it is **how the world functions**. The profession of hunting, capturing, studying, and breeding these creatures is as legitimate and necessary as blacksmithing or farming. Without the guilds that manage Planar Bleed fallout, trade routes would be cut off, villages would be overrun, and planar tears would go unstabilized.

---

## The Divine Context: Aon's Perspective

**Aíon** (god of stability and knowledge) views tragedy and chaos through a strategic lens: *"I knew you could succeed."*

His tolerance for Planar Bleed events, dangerous creatures, and world-shaking disasters is rooted in a fundamental belief: **epic stories of survival and adaptation stabilize the setting's metaphysical foundation**. The creatures that emerge from rifts are living proof of adaptability, and the heroes who face them strengthen the world's narrative coherence.

This explains:
- Why Aíon permits the Planar Switchboard to exist (a lost plane of magic accessible only in fragments)
- Why both tragedy and triumph are equally necessary
- Why the guilds' work is sacred, not mercenary

# PART 2: CREATURE CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM

## The Legal-Distinct Creature Types

### Beast
**Normal, mundane creatures** untouched by major planar influence. Wolves, bears, eagles, mundane insects. The baseline fauna of the material plane.

### Arcaniform
**Beasts and humanoids warped and empowered by magic.** Still recognizable as their original species, but enhanced. Examples: wolf with bark-skin and root-control, human mage whose body has absorbed so much aetheric energy that they've taken on crystalline traits. The magical creatures of the setting that remain tied to their origin.

### Abhorrent
**Entirely new and unique creatures formed from the raw chaotic energy of planar bleed.** They no longer resemble or belong to their original species. Cannot be "corrected" with magic without extreme complications. Examples: Eidolons, Vespidans, Cephalarchs. These are the truly alien creatures born from rift convergence.

### Forged
**Artificially created beings**—golems, constructs, magi-tech automata, clay-and-rune creations. Intentionally designed rather than evolved.

### Interstice
**Beings native to other distinct planes of existence.** Elemental planes, heavens, hells, pocket dimensions. Temporarily visiting, summoned, or displaced from their native plane. They are not bleed-affected; they are literal aliens from other realities.

### Floraform
**Plant creatures, whether naturally sentient or awakened by planar energy.** Includes ambulatory trees, carnivorous vines, fungal hives. Can be rooted or mobile.

### Chimeric
**Creatures that blend humanoid and bestial features in equal measure.** Distinct from Arcaniform because they are balanced hybrids, not empowered animals. Examples: centaurs, harpies, satyrs. **May originate from planar influence acting on humanoid and beast simultaneously, OR a humanoid exposed to planar influence taking on bestial traits, OR a beast gaining humanoid features.**

### Returned
**Non-living, reanimated, or incorporeal creatures.** Zombies, skeletons, ghosts, wraiths, constructs given animating spirits. Includes creatures that are fundamentally undead or liminal in nature.

## Multi-Axis Classification Framework

Every creature is classified across **multiple independent axes** to provide nuance and legal clarity for guild operations.

### Sapience Axis (Tiered System)

**Mindless** — No self-awareness. Acts purely on magical automation or base reflex. Examples: summoned swarms, enchanted objects, true zombies.

**Instinctual** — Acts on base instincts (hunger, territorial defense, mating) but has no sense of self. Can learn and adapt to stimuli. Examples: most beasts, some aberrations.

**Sentient** — Self-aware, capable of basic thought and simple emotions. Can recognize individuals, remember past interactions, communicate simple ideas. Examples: intelligent animals, some humanoid creatures of limited intellect.

**Sapient** — Capable of complex abstract thought, language, moral reasoning, long-term planning, and a sophisticated sense of self. Examples: humanoids, sapient Aberrants, high-intelligence Interstice.

**Legal Impact**: Capturing a sapient creature requires **informed consent or legal authorization**. A sapient creature treated as property can be prosecuted as kidnapping. Different kingdoms have different protections.

### Danger Index (Scaled System)

Modeled on natural disaster classification. Allows easy understanding of threat level.

| Rating | Category | Scope | Examples | Guild Response |
|--------|----------|-------|----------|-----------------|
| 1-3 | **Minor** | Threat to an individual | Small common beasts, weak constructs | Single ranger or small team |
| 4-6 | **Moderate** | Threat to a small group | Medium predators, unstable Aberrants | Team of 4-8 operatives |
| 7-9 | **Major** | Threat to a city or region | Powerful Eidolons, catastrophic Interstice | Guild mobilization, college support |
| 10 | **Catastrophic** | Threat to the world | Plane-breaching entities, reality-warping creatures | Multi-guild alliance, government intervention, planar sealing rituals |

**Danger is NOT intelligence.** A dim-witted but massive creature might be Catastrophic (10). A sapient but frail creature might be Minor (1-3). These are measured separately.

### Origin Axis (Where the creature came from)

**Material Plane Native** — Originally from the material plane before planar bleed. Most common origin.

**Primary Planar Influence** — Affected most strongly by one plane (fire, law, chaos, death, etc.). Shapes their resistances, abilities, and philosophy.

**Secondary/Tertiary Planar Influences** — Additional planar forces shape the creature further. Rare creatures influenced by 4+ planes are unpredictable and dangerous.

**Palimpsest-Touched** — Infused with Dust from the Palimpsest (lost plane of magic). These creatures exist outside normal fate constraints and are immune to planar switchboard penalties. Extremely rare and valuable.

## Planar Elements & Magical Corruption

### What Are Planar Elements?

**Planar Elements** are concentrated forms of **planar essence**—crystallized, weaponized, or harvested magical potential that can be incorporated into creatures or used as reagents for spell-crafting.

They exist in multiple categories:

#### Physical Forms
- **Elemental Hearts** (organ-like structures containing planar force)
- **Aetheric Ore** (metals infused with specific planar resonance)
- **Planar Flora** (plants that grew in regions of concentrated bleed)
- **Crystalline Shards** (quartz-like formations that trapped planar energy)
- **Essence Dust** (fine particulate matter—the most versatile form)

#### Sources of Planar Elements

**The Borderlands** — Regions where rifts frequently open, creating overlapping planar influences. Produces 44+ combinations of elemental and planar purity depending on which planes intersect.

**The Palimpsest Dust** — Harvested from the edges of the lost plane of magic. Creatures and objects infused with this dust exist outside the normal confines of fate and take no penalty or issues with planar switchboard interactions. Extraordinarily rare.

**Catalyst Sands** — Crystalline particles from areas of intense, prolonged planar bleed. Color and properties vary by source plane (red from fire planes, grey from law planes, etc.). Universal reagent that transmutes into magical components. Enabled mass production of magical items and replaced the need to hunt rare components.

**Magical Ley Lines** — Less common but stable sources. Some scholars hypothesize a lost plane of magic—they're not entirely wrong. The Planar Switchboard's influence leaks raw potential through the ley lines. **Note:** The two gods of magic have no access to the Switchboard; they are sustained by the leakage itself.

### Planar Corruption & Phenotype Development

#### The Genotype/Phenotype Model

**Base Genotype** = The creature's original form before planar exposure. Defines the skeletal structure, basic temperament, and ecological niche.

**Planar Influences** (mutagens) = Act on the genotype to create unique variations. The exact combination, intensity, and timing of planar exposure creates the **phenotype**—the creature's outward manifestation, abilities, and magical properties.

#### How Planar Influences Shape Form

A creature exposed to planar elements develops:
- **Color shifts and bioluminescence** (based on planar affinity)
- **New body parts or adaptations** (wings, armor plating, crystalline growths, limb mutations)
- **Behavioral changes** (temperament, aggression, predatory preferences)
- **Ability acquisition** (elemental breath, planar attunement, spatial distortion)
- **Symbiotic changes** (new organs, parasite relationships, fusion with aetheric constructs)

#### Chimera Development (Three Pathways)

**Pathway 1: Simultaneous Exposure** — A planar influence acts on a humanoid and a beast simultaneously, fusing them into a balanced hybrid. Creates centaurs, harpies, chimeras as equal blends.

**Pathway 2: Humanoid Becomes Bestial** — A humanoid is exposed to planar influence (especially bestial or feral planes) and takes on animal features while retaining humanoid cognition. Creates satyrs, merfolk, minotaurs.

**Pathway 3: Beast Becomes Humanoid** — A beast is exposed to planar influence (especially law or civilization planes) and gains humanoid features and intellect. Creates intelligent lupine creatures, avian humanoids, etc.

# PART 3: THE PROFESSION & GUILDS

## What Rangers/Field Agents Are

These are **professional hunters, handlers, and researchers** who manage the fallout from planar bleed. They are not "adventurers"—they have specific, defined roles like military MOS or contractor specialties.

### Professional Roles (All Names TBD)

**Tracker/Scout**
- Field intelligence, sign-reading, spoor analysis
- Planar residue mapping, habitat study
- Route prediction and migration tracking
- Works primarily alone or in pairs

**Field Handler**
- Live capture, conditioning, bond mediation
- Transport behavior management
- Lawful reagent harvesting from freshly-killed or molted creatures
- Hands-on expert who brings creatures in alive or breaks them down cleanly when lethal force is required

**Breaker/Suppressor**
- Neutralization of aggressive/rogue creatures
- Non-lethal or least-lethal combat as first priority
- Traps, shock-lines, ward nets, deterrent magic
- Lethal force only on authorization from guild magistrate

**Resonance & Containment Specialist**
- Operates Concord Crystals, aether stabilizers, rift-sealing devices
- Tunes crystals to creature planar signatures
- Erects mobile wards and temporary containment barriers
- Certifies holding cells and cages for transport
- Manages **stabilization** after capture (planar resonance re-establishment, counter-reagent application, hazard mitigation)

**Field Medic/Hex-Care**
- Treats team injuries (mundane and magical)
- Curse mitigation, poison/toxin treatment
- Exposure protocols for planar-corrupted wounds
- Revival of petrified, crystallized, or aetheric-burned team members

**Field Biologist**
- On-site triage and ecology documentation
- Necropsy and post-mortem analysis
- Harvesting organs, scales, crystals for crafting and research
- Chain-of-custody to colleges, casters, alchemists
- Documents mutation vectors and planar influence markers

**Diplomat/Envoy**
- Land rights negotiations with local holders and nobility
- Peace talks with sapient creatures or tribal groups
- Contract/bonding terms with sapient Aberrants seeking employment
- Calms panicked populations after creature sightings
- Handles ethical and legal compliance with local kingdoms

**Quartermaster**
- Supply, equipment, rationing, magical reagent stores
- Ammunition, ward components, healing supplies
- On-site logistics during field operations
- Inventory tracking and cost management

**Generalist Operative**
- Jack-of-all-trades utility role
- Can fill in as courier, scout, backup handler, trap-setter, mechanic
- Plugs gaps mid-operation
- Often cross-trained in multiple specialties

### Typical Field Team Composition

5-8 operatives drawn from the roles above, based on mission parameters. A creature hunt might require: Tracker, Field Handler, Breaker, Resonance Specialist, Field Biologist, Medic. A diplomatic negotiation: Diplomat, Tracker, Medic. A routine patrol: Scout, Generalist, Medic.

## Guild Structure: Three Layers

Guilds operate across **three interconnected tiers**, each with distinct function and authority.

### Tier 1: The Corporate Body

- **Presence**: Major cities, regional capitals
- **Role**: Funding, logistics, legal authority, licensing, magistrate offices
- **Funding Sources**: Mix of local governance, college sponsorship, and private investments
- **Loyalty**: To corporate interests AND public safety mandate
- **Authority**: Issues handler certs, containment certs, harvest endorsements, route clearances
- **Operations**: Long-term contracts, breeding programs, research projects, strategic planar breach response

### Tier 2: The Way Stations (Local Hubs)

- **Presence**: Across all inhabited lands—small towns, caravan routes, borderlands
- **Role**: Operational anchors, not corporate control
- **Structure**: One-stop-shop providing:
  - **Tavern/Inn** for food, rest, recovery
  - **Guild Hall** with job boards, mission payouts, team marshaling
  - **Apothecary/Alchemy** for healing supplies, poisons, magical materials requisitions
  - **Lab/Forge** for item creation, maintenance, magical and mundane repairs
  - **Garden** (or equivalent) for aesthetics and renewable food production
- **Funding**: Supported by corporate body + local governance + guild profits
- **Staffing**: Locally hired personnel, limited central authority
- **Authority**: Can accept local contracts, issue temporary permits, manage day-to-day operations

### Tier 3: The Shell Game (Politics & Inter-Guild Competition)

- **Presence**: Government chambers, college councils, nobility courts
- **Role**: Political maneuvering, funding allocation, resource competition
- **Players**: Not smaller organizations, but **major political actors**—rival guilds, government factions, college schools, noble houses
- **Function**: Competing for:
  - Exclusive hunting rights to valuable creatures
  - Government contracts for planar breach response
  - College sponsorship and research access
  - Territory and way-station licensing
- **Impact on PCs**: Political struggles affect mission availability, funding, rivalry dynamics

## Guild Funding & Multi-Financing

A single guild can simultaneously be:
- Funded by a college (research mandate)
- Funded by local government (public safety)
- Funded by a noble house (private menagerie management)
- Funded by private investors (profit-seeking)

This creates **competing loyalties and philosophical tensions**. A guild might be ideologically aligned with the Wildpath Wardens Academy (containment and control) but financially dependent on the College of Conjuration (exploitation and commerce). This leads to internal conflict and interesting story friction.

### The Generalist College as Great Equalizer

The **Generalist College** functions as:
- **Neutral mediator** ensuring multi-financed teams don't clash on core ethics
- **Source of expertise**, providing training and knowledge to all guilds regardless of affiliation
- **Regulator**, ensuring guilds adhere to legal and ethical standards
- **Clearinghouse**, matching contracts to appropriate teams and vetting guild conduct

## Why Guilds Exist (The Practical Case)

Without guilds:
- Planar rifts would go undetected and unseal
- Dangerous creatures would overrun settlements
- Trade routes would be cut off by aberrations
- Planar bleed fallout would cause cascading magical disasters
- Sapient creatures would be hunted or exploited without legal protection

With guilds:
- Rifts are monitored, mapped, and sealed
- Creatures are managed (contained, relocated, studied, or culled)
- Supply chains for planar materials are stable and legal
- Planar breaches are responded to rapidly
- Society can function despite a world constantly exposed to magical instability

**Guilds are not optional. They are infrastructure.**

## What Decontamination Actually Is

**Critical Clarification:** Decontamination in this system is **NOT radiation scrubbing**. It is **stabilization + hazard mitigation** of planar mutations.

### The Nature of Planar Adaptation

When a creature is exposed to planar influence, the transformation is:
- **Instantaneous** (not gradual)
- **Irreversible without intervention** (like rust/corrosion—can't scrub it away)
- **Adaptive** (the creature has survived by taking on these traits; they help it thrive in a mixed-planar environment)
- **Structurally integrated** (the mutations are now part of the creature's physiology and magic)

There is **no surface contamination to wipe clean**. The creature's body and aura have fundamentally changed. What guilds call "decon" is actually managing the aftermath and preventing further uncontrolled mutation.

### Decontamination Process

**Decontamination has two phases:**

#### Phase 1: Immediate Stabilization (First 1-6 hours post-capture)

**Goal:** Stop active planar mutation and lock the creature's current form in place.

**Methods:**
- **Counter-Reagent Application** — Administer specific Planar Elements (often opposing the creature's primary influence) to prevent further drift. Fire-exposed creatures get cold-aligned essence to stabilize. Law-aligned creatures get chaos-aligned catalysts.
- **Temporary Ward Networks** — Deploy runes that isolate the creature from ambient planar radiation and bleed effects. Create "clean zones" in holding cells.

**Duration:** 4-12 hours depending on creature size and planar intensity.

#### Phase 2: Extended Stabilization & Hazard Mitigation (Days 2-14 post-capture)

**Goal:** Prevent cascading mutations, lock beneficial adaptations, isolate dangerous effects.

**Methods:**
- **Continued Counter-Reagent Dosing** — Regular application of stabilizing essences to maintain planar equilibrium. Creates a new "set point" for the creature's mutations.
- **Behavioral Observation** — Monitor for signs of cascading corruption or dangerous ability development.
- **Specialized Counter-Rituals** — If a creature shows signs of runaway planar influence, perform binding rituals that lock specific mutations in place.

**Duration:** Variable based on creature stability. Stable creatures may only need 2-3 days; volatile ones need 2+ weeks.

### Hazard Mitigation Specifics

Not all hazards are magical. Decontamination also means:

- **Isolating Magically Reactive Gear** — Remove any items the creature interacts with in unexpected ways. A fire-aligned creature might superheat metal weapons. An entropy-aligned creature might destabilize healing potions.
- **Environmental Shielding** — Create rune-inscribed containment cages customized to the creature's type:
  - **Fire-aligned creatures**: Rune of intense cold, water-infused walls, chill auras
  - **Spell-casting creatures**: Magical damping runes, anti-magic fields
  - **Incorporeal creatures**: Binding circles, ectoplasm-reactive barriers
  - **Symbiotic creatures**: Isolation chambers to prevent host-seeking behavior
- **Handling Hazardous Flesh** — Some creature organs, blood, or tissues remain magically active and dangerous. These are harvested with protective gear and stored separately.

### Physical Transport During & After Stabilization

**Transport vehicles** are heavily modified for creature containment:

**Standard Creature Wagon**
- Reinforced wooden frame with iron-banded axles (drawn by rune-tracked golem or destrier teams)
- Interior: rune-lined wooden compartments, 8-12 feet long
- Rune-locking mechanisms with multiple redundant locks
- Shock-absorbing suspension to minimize creature distress

**High-Danger Containment Cart**
- Mobile anti-magic cage (a Faraday-cage equivalent using rune-networks)
- Completely sealed, armored exterior
- Multiple containment redundancies
- Rune-locks that require magistrate authorization to open
- Atmosphere control for creatures that require specific planar conditions

**Specialized Transports**
- **Chill-Wagons** for fire-aligned creatures (cooled via constant Cold spell maintenance)
- **Silence-Carts** for spell-casters (magical sound-dampening runes)
- **Tether-Sleds** for flying creatures (aerial containment via magical anchors)

When a creature is killed or molts, the Field Biologist harvests legally protected materials. These become:

1. **Magical components** for spell-crafting
2. **Reagents** for alchemical work
3. **Crafting materials** for magi-tech construction
4. **Research specimens** for colleges

### Categories of Harvestable Materials

#### Elemental Hearts
- **What**: Organ-like structures containing concentrated planar force
- **Source**: Aberrants, Interstice, high-level Arcaniform
- **Uses**: Powering artifacts, crafting magical weapons, enhancing spells
- **Example**: Heart of a Cinder Salamander (grants fire-aligned mutations when applied to creatures)
- **Legality**: Highly regulated; different kingdoms have different harvest restrictions

#### Aetheric Ore
- **What**: Metals infused with planar resonance (crystalline iron, shadow-silver, void-steel)
- **Source**: Forged creatures, ore-based Minerals, metallically-aligned Aberrants
- **Uses**: Crafting magical armor, weapons, Concord Crystals, runework
- **Example**: Whispersilver (ore from a planar bleed of law + void planes; usable for anti-magic containment)

#### Planar Flora
- **What**: Plants that grew in bleed zones; retain planar properties
- **Source**: Borderlands, gardens near rift sites, Floraform creature harvests
- **Uses**: Alchemy, potion-brewing, incense for rituals, reagents for transmutation
- **Example**: Voidbark (plant from a void-plane bleed; used in potions of transposition)
- **Legality**: Often freely available but of variable quality depending on harvest location

#### Crystalline Shards & Essence Dust
- **What**: Quartz-like formations that trapped planar energy; fine particulate matter
- **Source**: Borderlands, rift sites, from creature corpses
- **Uses**: Spell-crafting, potion-making, universal reagent for alchemy, Concord Crystal construction
- **Example**: Catalyst Sands (the most common and valuable form; enabled industrialization of magic)
- **Legality**: Partially regulated; mass-produced and widely available but high-quality sources are controlled

#### The Borderlands
Regions where rifts open frequently, creating overlapping planar influences. Produces:
- 44+ combinations of elemental purity and planar type (depending on which planes overlap)

#### Palimpsest Dust
The truly exotic material:
- Harvested from the edges of the lost plane of magic
- Creatures infused with Palimpsest Dust exist **outside normal fate constraints**
- Immune to planar switchboard penalties
- Extraordinarily rare (only guild magistrates and colleges have access)
- Rumors suggest some mortals are attempting to artificially synthesize it (dangerous)

#### Catalyst Sands
The industrial backbone:
- Crystalline particles from areas of intense, prolonged planar bleed
- Color/properties vary by source plane
- Universal reagent that transmutes into magical components
- Replaced the need to hunt rare components
- Enabled mass production of magical items
- Core to modern magi-punk infrastructure
- Widely available but still valuable; prices fluctuate with supply

- Less common but stable sources
- Some scholars hypothesize a lost plane of magic—they're correct
- The Planar Switchboard's influence leaks raw potential through ley lines
- **Critical Note:** The two gods of magic (not Aíon) have no access to the Switchboard; they are sustained by the leakage itself
- Creating new ley lines or tapping existing ones is extremely dangerous and typically forbidden

## PART 7: CAPTURE MECHANICS (OPTIONS & FRAMEWORK)

---

## Critical Principle: Capture is not "Catch"

In this system, **capture is a specialization**. You do not throw a ball and catch a creature. Instead, you use:

- **Magical tools** designed around planar resonance
- **Combat suppression** (subduing without killing)
- **Ritual binding** (for high-danger creatures)
- **Negotiation & consent** (for sapient creatures)
- **Environmental manipulation** (traps, barriers, lures)

There is **no single universal capture method**. A Nuisance (1-3 danger) creature might be caught with a simple tool. A Catastrophic (10 danger) creature might require a week of preparation, a full guild team, and a government-sanctioned binding ritual.

---

## The Three Pillars of Capture

### Pillar 1: Subdual (Get the Creature Weak Enough to Contain)

Before you can capture, the creature must be:
- **Unconscious** (via tranquilizers, magic, exhaustion)
- **Restrained** (via physical or magical bonds)
- **Helpless** (via paralysis, petrification, or magical compulsion)
- **Willing** (if sapient and requiring consent)

**Subdual Methods (Examples - More Below):**
- Tranquilizing darts/arrows with creature-specific sedatives
- Combat and tactical suppression
- Magical compulsion spells (Dominate, Command, Hold Person/Monster)
- Environmental hazards (fire, cold, barriers that weaken the creature)
- Traps and snares
- Negotiation and binding contracts (for sapient creatures)
- Ward networks that exhaust the creature's aetheric reserves

### Pillar 2: Binding (Lock the Creature in Place)

Once subdued, the creature must be bound such that it **cannot escape**. Binding options:

- **Physical Restraint**: Rune-charged chains, anti-magic cages, magical locks
- **Aetheric Anchoring**: Runes or artifacts that bind the creature to a location or container
- **Consciousness Suppression**: Spells that keep the creature unconscious for extended periods
- **Consent Contract**: For sapient creatures, a binding agreement (magically enforced) defining the terms

### Pillar 3: Containment (Keep the Creature Secured Until Transfer)

The creature must be transported securely from capture site to destination. Containment methods:

- **Physical Cages**: Reinforced, rune-lined holding cells with multiple locks
- **Anti-Magic Chambers**: Faraday-cage-equivalent containers that suppress the creature's magical abilities
- **Sedation Maintenance**: Regular doses of tranquilizer to keep the creature docile
- **Transport Vehicles**: Specialized wagons designed for creature hauling (see Part 4)

## THE TOOLKIT: Capture Devices & Methods

You've indicated that you want **options**, not a single prescribed method. Here are the core frameworks:

### OPTION SET A: Binding Crystals

**The Concept:**
Specialized crystals that resonate with a creature's planar signature. The crystal acts as:
- A **beacon** (attracts the creature or binds to it)
- A **container** (holds the creature's essence/body)
- A **lock** (prevents escape via planar resistance)

#### Binding Oath & Magical Contract
- **Preparation**: A binding contract written out, detailing the terms of capture, duration, and constraints. Requires only a few minutes.
- **Function**: A sapient creature agrees to the terms (or is magically compelled). Violation of terms causes magical backlash (pain, weakened abilities, magical exhaustion).
- **Activation**: The creature signs or verbally agrees. Both parties are bound.
- **Containment**: The creature is magically compelled to obey the contract. Escape is possible but incurs magical penalties.
- **Limits**:
  - Only works on sapient creatures (or requires magical domination)
  - Circumvention possible (clever wording loopholes, but risky)
  - Contract can be broken by higher magic (wish, miracle, divine intervention)
  - Requires both parties' agreement or magical compulsion

---
## The Consent Question: Binding Agreements

For **sapient creatures**, capture is not force—it's negotiation.

**Types of Sapient Creature Agreements:**

1. **Employment Contract** — Creature agrees to work for a handler/guild for defined period and pay
2. **Breeding Program Agreement** — Creature agrees to parentage in exchange for protection/resources
3. **Research Consent** — Creature agrees to be studied in exchange for safety and payment
4. **Bonding Compact** — Creature agrees to psychic/magical bond with handler for mutual benefit
5. **Service Term** — Creature agrees to temporary capture for training/conditioning, then release

All agreements are **magically binding** (Oath magic). Breaking the agreement incurs consequences:
- Magical exhaustion
- Compulsive pain
- Weakened abilities
- Forced return to location/handler

This means **sapient creatures have rights** under guild law. Exploiting or breaking a binding agreement with a sapient creature can result in guild sanctions, legal prosecution, or loss of license.

# Bonding & Handler Relationships

## What Bonding Is (And Isn't)

**Bonding is not a magical enslavement or trainer-Pokémon relationship.** It is a **mutual pact** between handler and creature that creates psychological and sometimes magical benefits.

### Types of Bonds

#### Bond Type 1: Utilitarian Bond
- **What**: The creature recognizes the handler as owner/provider but has no deep emotional investment
- **How it Starts**: Prolonged proximity, consistent feeding, habituation
- **Handler Benefit**: Creature will return to handler; obeys basic commands; won't flee
- **Creature Benefit**: Food, shelter, protection, social structure
- **Bond Strength**: Weak; creature may leave if better offer appears
- **Magical Component**: None required (purely behavioral)
- **Examples**: Captured beast used for labor; creature working for pay

#### Bond Type 2: Affection Bond
- **What**: The creature develops genuine emotional attachment to the handler
- **How it Starts**: Consistent care, protection, positive reinforcement, time investment (weeks to months)
- **Handler Benefit**: Creature prioritizes handler's safety; improved combat performance alongside handler; creature may warn handler of danger
- **Creature Benefit**: All utilitarian benefits + emotional security, companionship
- **Bond Strength**: Moderate to strong; creature will resist separation
- **Magical Component**: Can be enhanced with magical rituals (Enchantment magic)
- **Examples**: Handler who genuinely cares for a creature; sapient creatures that trust their handler

#### Bond Type 3: Psychic Bond (Rare & Dangerous)
- **What**: A magical link between handler and creature's mind. Handler can sense creature's location, emotions, and basic thoughts. Creature can communicate telepathically with handler.
- **How it Starts**: Ritual bonding (requires both parties' willing participation or magical compulsion); typically only attempted with high-intelligence creatures
- **Handler Benefit**: 
  - Sense creature's location and emotional state at all times
  - Telepathic communication (up to 1 mile range)
  - Enhanced coordination in combat
  - Can share senses briefly (see through creature's eyes)
- **Creature Benefit**: 
  - Handler can share memories (helps creature learn)
  - Handler can calm creature telepathically
  - Handler's willpower can bolster creature's resistance
- **Bond Strength**: Very strong; creature feels handler's death as psychic pain; forced separation causes psychological damage
- **Magical Component**: **YES—requires ritual magic and both parties' magical signature**
- **Risk**: 
  - If handler dies, creature may enter psychological breakdown or berserker state
  - If creature dies, handler suffers psychic backlash
  - Abuse of bond (through psychic manipulation) can cause permanent damage to both parties
- **Examples**: Elite hunters with their signature creatures; sapient companions with deep mutual trust

# Mutation & Planar Customization System

## The Genotype/Phenotype Model (In Practice)

### Baseline Creature (Genotype)
A captured creature has a **base form and base abilities** determined by its species and planar origin.

**Example**: A dire wolf captured from the borderlands has:
- Base intelligence (animal instinct)
- Base strength/speed (natural predator)
- Base resistance (natural fur/hide)
- No elemental abilities or special mutations

### Planar Element Application (Phenotype Modification)

Planar elements can be **applied to creatures** to grant new mutations. This is the core of creature customization.

#### How Application Works

1. **Element is harvested** from a Borderlands region or harvested from another creature
2. **Field Biologist examines the element** to determine its planar affinity (fire, water, law, chaos, etc.)
3. **Handler and creature are prepared** for the ritual (both must be willing or creature is magically dominated)
4. **Ritual is performed** (takes 1-8 hours depending on element power)
5. **Element is incorporated** into creature's biology via planar resonance

#### Result of Successful Application

The creature's phenotype shifts:
- **Physical changes**: Color shift, new appendages, crystalline growths, scale development
- **Ability acquisition**: Elemental breathing, new magical resistance, new special attack
- **Behavioral shift**: Personality may change subtly; creature becomes more attuned to the element's philosophy
- **Capability increase**: Creature's overall power increases (danger rating may increase)

#### Risk of Failed Application

If the ritual fails:
- Creature suffers **Aetheric Burnout** (magical exhaustion, temporary incapacity)
- Some mutations may partially manifest (instability; creature becomes harder to control)
- Element is wasted
- Creature may require weeks of recovery

### Mutation Tracks (Optional Structured System)

**You can use a "track" system** to organize creature mutations mechanically. Each track represents a progression of mutations tied to a planar affinity.

**Example: Fire Affinity Track**

| Stage | Mutation | Ability Gained | Change |
|-------|----------|---|--|
| 1 (Imbuement) | Ember-Hide | Gain fire resistance; coat slight heat aura | Red/orange coloration develops |
| 2 (Integration) | Flame-Form | Can ignite enemies on touch; breathing becomes hot | Scales replace fur; eyes glow |
| 3 (Fusion) | Inferno-Bond | Can breathe fire once per day; immune to cold; double damage to ice | Body radiates intense heat; wings may develop |
| 4 (Transcendence) | Elemental Avatar | Can cast fireball-equivalent spell; can temporarily become fire; fire creatures recognize them as kin | Barely recognizable as original creature |

**Each stage requires a specific planar element harvested from a fire-aligned source.** Progress down the track only if handler applies new elements.

### Corruption Threshold (The Dark Side)

If a creature receives **too many planar elements too quickly** (more than 3 major mutations in 6 months), or if they're exposed to **conflicting planar forces** (fire + cold, law + chaos), they may become **Corrupted**.

**Corruption Manifests As:**
- Unstable mutations (random ability activations)
- Behavioral degradation (creature becomes feral or insane)
- Rapid aging or physical deterioration
- Loss of handler bond (creature no longer recognizes handler)
- Potential transformation into an Aberrant (irreversible)

## Breeding & Hereditary Traits

Two creatures can breed naturally if:
- Both are sapient or handlers facilitate breeding behavior
- Both are physiologically compatible (same species or closely related)
- Breeding season/pheromone conditions are right

**Offspring inherit:**
- Base genotype of both parents (random Mendelian inheritance)
- Some (not all) of parents' phenotypical mutations
- Blend of parents' temperaments

### Intentional Breeding Program

Guild-run breeding programs aim to create creatures with **specific traits**.

**Goal Examples:**
- Fire-breathing dire wolves for siege work
- Law-aligned Aberrants for guard duty
- Sapient creatures for diplomatic work
- Miniaturized versions for spy work

**Methods:**
- Select parents with desired mutations
- Apply specific planar elements to parents to "lock in" traits
- Breed offspring
- Offspring naturally inherit locked-in mutations
- Apply additional elements to offspring for new traits

### Genetic Stability Issues

Over-breeding or breeding unstable creatures leads to **genetic collapse**:
- Offspring are sickly or stillborn
- Offspring are infertile
- Mutations fail to express
- Offspring develop corruption faster

This is why **wild-caught creatures are sometimes preferable to bred ones**—natural populations maintain genetic diversity.

## Communication & Intelligence Development

### Natural Communication

**Non-sapient creatures communicate via:**
- Vocalizations (roars, chirps, songs)
- Body language (posture, movement)
- Pheromones (scent marks, mating calls)
- Ritualized behavior (threat displays, play-fighting)

**Handlers learn to read these signals** through experience and training.

### Taught Communication (Imprinting)

Young creatures that imprint on humanoid handlers **naturally absorb human language**. They may:
- Understand simple commands (go, attack, sit, stop)
- Understand named objects (handler's name, "food," "danger")
- Eventually speak (if mouth structure allows)
- Develop rudimentary concepts (mine vs. yours, safe vs. danger)

This is **not magical**—it's the same way a dog learns human language.

### Sapient Communication

Sapient creatures **already have language and complex thought**. They:
- Speak in full sentences
- Understand abstract concepts (law, honor, profit, love)
- Negotiate and make deals
- Lie and deceive
- Develop cultural and political positions

### Magical Enhancement

**Handlers can magically enhance communication:**
- **Enchantment magic** to improve creature's understanding of human language
- **Sending spell** variant to telepathically communicate with creature at distance
- **Comprehend Languages** spell to understand creature's native speech
- **Tongues spell** to allow creature to speak handler's language perfectly

These are all expensive and typically only used for high-value creatures or sapient companions.

## Lore: Creature Creation & the Three Great Aberrants

### The Origin of Eidolons, Vespidans, & Cephalarchs

These three species **did not exist before planar bleed**. They are the primary examples of **Abhorrent-type creatures**: entirely new lifeforms born from the interaction of planar forces.

#### Eidolons (Planar Echoes)
- **Origin**: Emerged from overlapping law + magic planes. Born from concepts made flesh.
- **Nature**: Partially incorporeal; exist partially in material plane and partially in planar space
- **Sapience**: High (capable of abstract thought, communication, moral reasoning)
- **Danger**: Variable (7-10, depending on individual power)
- **Special Trait**: Can manifest as solid or partially-transparent. Can affect objects from planar space without physical contact.
- **Society**: Unknown; some Eidolons are solitary, others form loose collectives
- **Value to Guilds**: Extremely valuable for research; sapient contracts for specialized work

#### Vespidans (Hive-Mind Collective)
- **Origin**: Emerged from overlapping life + chaos planes. Insects exposed to chaotic vitality and grew sapient and organized.
- **Nature**: Eusocial collective; individual Vespidans are mildly sapient, but colony mind is highly intelligent
- **Sapience**: High (colony-level), Low-Moderate (individual level)
- **Danger**: 6-9 (collective action is highly coordinated)
- **Special Trait**: Can split/rejoin population (shed workers, rejoin colony). Colony can act as single entity or many.
- **Society**: Hierarchical hive structure with queens and castes
- **Value to Guilds**: Excellent for labor; can be contracted as work-crews; valuable for studying collective intelligence

#### Cephalarchs (Void-Touched Intelligences)
- **Origin**: Emerged from overlapping void + knowledge planes. Creatures that touched the edges of the lost plane of magic.
- **Nature**: Highly mutable; form shifts based on thought and will. Partially dream-like in manifestation.
- **Sapience**: Very high (possibly superhuman in certain domains)
- **Danger**: 8-10 (unpredictable; can manipulate planar laws)
- **Special Trait**: Can reshape themselves; can access planar memory (knowledge of events they never witnessed); partially immune to reality-based restrictions
- **Society**: Unknown; Cephalarchs rarely interact with other species
- **Value to Guilds**: Invaluable for research; dangerous to capture; nearly impossible to control

## Ethical & Legal Framework

### Guild Licensing & Ethics

**Guilds operate under a licensing system** that enforces ethical and legal standards:

**Handler Licensing Tiers:**

| Tier | Authority | Can Capture | Cannot Capture | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Novice** | Limited | Danger 1-3 (Nuisance) | Danger 4+ | Supervised by licensed handler |
| **Journeyman** | Standard | Danger 1-5 (Moderate) | Danger 6+ | Solo authority; approved contracts |
| **Master** | Full | Danger 1-9 (Major) | Danger 10 (Catastrophic) | Team leadership; training authority |
| **Magistrate** | Highest | All danger levels | None (subject to government approval) | Contract authority; legal prosecution; official government agent |

**Rights of Sapient Creatures:**

- Sapient creatures **cannot be captured without consent** (except government order)
- Sapient creatures **cannot be killed without trial** (legal standing in courts)
- Sapient creatures have **property rights** (can own items, territory)
- Sapient creatures can **sue for breach of contract** (magical binding agreements are enforceable)
- Sapient creatures **cannot be bred without consent** (reproduction is considered autonomous choice)

### Harvest Restrictions

**Certain creatures and materials are legally protected:**

- **Returning creatures** (undead) cannot be killed without specific authorization
- **Sapient creatures' organs** cannot be harvested (considers it murder)
- **Sentient creatures** (non-sapient but aware) have some protections (killing is regulated, not prohibited)
- **Protected species** (endangered or sacred creatures) cannot be hunted in certain regions

Violations result in:
- Loss of guild license
- Criminal prosecution
- Financial restitution to creature or creature's owner
- Imprisonment or exile
