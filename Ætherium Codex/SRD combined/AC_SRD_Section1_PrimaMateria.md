# THE ÆTHERIUM CODEX — System Reference Document
## Section I: Prima Materia — Framework & Principles

*Alpha SRD Draft — Working Document*

### PREAMBLE: THE INSEPARABILITY PRINCIPLE

An item's alchemical specification IS its mechanical specification. The dual periodic table entry for a material IS its stat block. The spell sentence specification for an effect IS its component register. The design space is discovered, not invented.

This means every number in the system is derivable from first principles. A designer does not decide that a steel longsword deals 1d8 damage — the real periodic table specifies that steel's tensile strength places it in that damage tier. An alchemist does not decide that obsidian has a Borderlands heritage — the material's volcanic origin determines its planar passport. The system reads the world; it does not impose upon it.

### PART I: THE FIVE PLANES

Five planar engines underlie all magical and material reality. Each plane is a complex system governing a domain of existence. None of them reduce to a single concept — they are engines with multiple outputs, internal dynamics, and characteristic behaviors.

**The Borderlands — The Engine of Matter**

The Borderlands governs physical substance and its transformations. Its domain includes elemental interaction, phase transition, material properties, environmental forces, and the behavior of matter under pressure, temperature, and chemical reaction. It encompasses fire, water, earth, air, and every state that emerges from their combinations — but it is not merely "the elements." It is the full physics of how matter behaves, reacts, persists, and transforms.

Phase Wheel: Earth (Solid) / Water (Liquid) / Air (Gas) / Fire (Plasma)
Diagonal Pairs (Competing States): Earth ↔ Air / Water ↔ Fire

**The Verdant Font — The Engine of Biological Process**

The Verdant Font governs biological systems in their full complexity: growth, adaptation, consumption, propagation, mutation, symbiosis, predation, parasitism, disease, immune response, and ecological interaction. It is not "life" in any simplified sense — it includes the fungal network consuming a dead tree, the virus hijacking a host cell, the predator consuming prey, and the cancer growing without regulation. The Font's domain is every process by which biological systems operate, regardless of whether the outcome is beneficial, harmful, or neutral to any particular organism.

Phase Wheel: Flora (Solid) / Myco (Liquid) / Viral (Gas) / Fauna (Plasma)
Diagonal Pairs (Competing States): Flora ↔ Viral / Myco ↔ Fauna

**The Synod of Endings — The Engine of Completion**

The Synod governs endings, preservation, permanence, information storage, identity persistence, and the process by which all systems reach their most stable final state. Its domain includes the physical remains after biological process ceases, the archival record of what existed, the spectral persistence of identity beyond physical function, and the entropic void that claims what cannot be preserved. The Synod absorbed the Silent Athenaeum — the older concept of forbidden knowledge and preserved secrets now exists as the Synod's Archival and Sepulchral expressions. The Synod is patient. It asserts that everything will eventually reach its most stable state. It is always correct.

Phase Wheel: Osseous (Solid) / Archival (Liquid) / Spectral (Gas) / Void (Plasma)
Diagonal Pairs (Competing States): Osseous ↔ Spectral / Archival ↔ Void

**The Hidden Forge — The Engine of Technology**

The Hidden Forge governs the processes by which incompatible things are made to work together. Its domain includes all forms of mediation, catalysis, engineering, and deliberate problem-solving through applied technique. The Forge has no internal antithetical states — making incompatible things compatible is its fundamental nature. All sixteen of its internal isotope combinations are natural. The Forge is the plane that holds the competing diagonal states of other planes in tension, and it is the catalyst required for cross-planar emulsions.

Phase Wheel: Crystal (Solid) / Chem (Liquid) / Pneuma (Gas) / Volt (Plasma)
Diagonal Pairs: None — all combinations are natural within the Forge's domain

**The Palimpsest — The Engine of Superposition**

The Palimpsest governs probability, narrative determination, and the space where choices exist before they are made. Its domain includes fate, luck, historical echo, temporal perception, and the process by which contested outcomes become resolved. The Palimpsest produces no physical effects on its own — every physically impactful Palimpsest expression requires a secondary plane to give the probability a body. What the Palimpsest determines is WHICH of the possible outcomes actually occurs. Mortals attribute the Palimpsest as the plane of magic, but it is more accurately the plane of being in superposition — the place where choices are literal representations and where unrealized possibilities still have form.

Phase Wheel: Echo/Resolved (Solid) / Veil/Handed (Liquid) / Probability/Contested (Gas) / Glitch/Suspended (Plasma)
Diagonal Pairs (Competing States): Echo ↔ Probability (Resolved ↔ Contested) / Veil ↔ Glitch (Handed ↔ Suspended)

### PART II: PLANAR INTERACTION TYPES

When planar expressions combine, the type of combination determines stability, crafting requirements, and mechanical behavior.

**◆ Natural Combinations**

Natural combinations form without external catalyst under appropriate conditions. The constituent expressions cooperate or at minimum coexist without requiring sustained input to maintain the combined state. Adjacent expressions on the phase wheel (e.g., Earth+Water, Fire+Air) are natural — they don't fight each other. All sixteen Hidden Forge internal combinations are natural.

**⚙ Catalyzed Diagonal Combinations**

Catalyzed diagonals are combinations of competing states within the same plane. Fire and Water compete — fire evaporates water, water extinguishes fire. Earth and Air compete — earth settles and consolidates, air disperses and lifts. These combinations can and do occur in nature (steam exists wherever heat meets water; lightning exists wherever atmospheric electricity discharges), but the state is inherently contested. It requires sustained energy input to maintain because the constituent states are actively trying to resolve to one side or the other. In nature, the environment provides that input. In crafting, Forge mediation provides it. The ⚙ designation tells the alchemist: this compound needs ongoing stabilization and will not persist passively.

**◆⚙ Emulsion Combinations**

Emulsions are combinations of expressions from antithetical planes — forced together by a Hidden Forge catalyst. These are cosmological events, not environmental conditions. The two antithetical pairs are:

- Verdant Font ↔ Synod of Endings (the Biotic Paradox)
- Borderlands ↔ Palimpsest (the Reality Paradox)

Emulsions require three-domain knowledge: both antithetical planes plus the Forge. This is why mastering emulsion work is the most difficult alchemical discipline — it demands understanding of three separate planar systems and how to force them into coexistence.

The catalyst type (Crystal, Chemical, Pneuma, or Volt) determines the character of how the antithetical tension is maintained, each with its own instability mode and failure behavior.

### PART III: THE ISOTOPE SYSTEM

Each plane contains sixteen isotope states — four pure expressions and twelve combinations formed from pairs of the four phase elements. These isotopes are the specific planar expressions that materials embody, that casters learn to work with, and that items manifest as mutations or installed effects.

An isotope is identified by its plane prefix and number:
- B-01 through B-16: Borderlands
- V-01 through V-16: Verdant Font
- S-01 through S-16: Synod of Endings
- F-01 through F-16: Hidden Forge
- P-01 through P-16: Palimpsest

Beyond the single-plane isotopes, there are:
- 16 Cross-Planar Natural States (CP-01 through CP-16): formed when two compatible planes bleed into the same space
- 8 Emulsion States (E-01 through E-08): formed when antithetical planes are forced together by Forge catalyst

**Isotope Profile Structure**

Every isotope profile contains:
- **Phase and Type:** Which phase elements compose it and whether it is ◆ Natural or ⚙ Catalyzed
- **Interaction Profile:** How the material's phase state behaves — Static, Cycling, Projecting, or Absorbing (derived from Solid, Liquid, Gas, Plasma respectively)
- **Natural Effects:** The mechanical effects the isotope can produce, with descriptions
- **Hard Exclusions:** Effects the isotope categorically cannot produce, regardless of Formation Stage or crafting skill
- **Formation Stage Shift:** How the isotope's expression changes across Formation Stages 1 through 5
- **Median Expression:** The default effect the isotope produces if installed or expressed without further specification

**Learning Isotopes**

A caster learns individual isotopes as discrete talents. Each learned isotope grants the caster the ability to produce effects from that isotope's profile. Pure elements (the four cardinal expressions of each plane) are prerequisite foundations — a caster must understand the constituent pures before they can work with compound isotopes. Catalyzed diagonal isotopes require additional understanding of Forge mediation. Emulsion isotopes require knowledge from two antithetical planes plus the Forge.

This structure means casters naturally specialize. A practitioner who has learned B-01 (Inferno), B-04 (Scorch), and B-14 (Current) has a very different capability profile from one who has learned B-05 (The Abyss), B-07 (Mire), and B-08 (Hail) — even though both are "Borderlands practitioners." Mastery is distributed, not monolithic.

### PART IV: THE THREE MATERIAL LAYERS

Every item in the system has up to three distinct layers. These layers are separate in origin, function, and mechanical behavior. They do not occupy the same design space.

**Layer 1: Base Material (Physical Properties)**

The base material is the real-world substance the item is made from. Its properties derive from the real periodic table — hardness from Mohs scale, damage tier from tensile strength, weight class from density, chemical behavior from reactivity. A base material is not magical. Steel is hard, holds an edge, and conducts heat. Obsidian is sharp, brittle, and chemically inert. Bone is light, slightly flexible, and porous. These are physical facts read from the real world and translated into system statistics.

The base material also has an alchemical passport — the reading from the alchemical periodic table that identifies the material's planar heritage and compatibility. This passport is information. It tells the alchemist what the material WAS (obsidian was magma), what it is COMPATIBLE with (Borderlands expressions align naturally), and what its Formation Stage is. The passport does not grant effects. It grants knowledge.

**Layer 2: Mutation (Innate Planar Expression)**

A mutation is a planar influence expressing itself in the material. It is not installed by a crafter — it occurs through planar bleed, environmental exposure, or the material's own history. A mutation does not occupy a slot. It is what the material IS at the planar level.

Every material with planar exposure expresses something — the expression is never truly zero, though at low intensity (EML 1) it may be cosmetic or functionally negligible. A mutation can grant spell-like abilities, passive effects, or behavioral characteristics, all built through the spell sentence grammar and constrained by the relevant isotope's effect profile and hard exclusions.

The mutation generates its own charges for its own effects through the drip recharge system, at a rate determined by the material's Formation Stage and planar character. This self-generation powers the mutation's innate effects and nothing else — it cannot fuel installed inset components.

**Layer 3: Inset Components (Installed Effects)**

Inset components are removable magical or mechanical additions installed into the item's slot architecture. They are crafted, installed, and can be removed or replaced by a practitioner with appropriate skill. Each slot is binary — empty or occupied by exactly one component.

Inset components require external power from a battery component housed in a dedicated power housing (not counted against the main slot total). The battery's recharge behavior is determined by its own planar character and Way expression, following the drip recharge system.

Between the mutation layer and the inset layer sits the **additive layer** — physical attachments (hilts, grips, wraps, guards, scabbards, inlays, fittings) that carry their own alchemical treatments and modify the interaction between the item's existing expressions and the physical world. Additives do not occupy inset slots. They are alchemical treatments applied to manage, direct, inhibit, or channel the item's behavior — making a raw mutated material into a functional, usable object.

### PART V: MASTERY AND SCALE

Mastery is rated 1 through 10 for mortal beings and items. Mastery 10 represents the peak of human capability. Spells and items can technically be constructed above Mastery 10, but require anchors or mechanisms that bring their effective requirements within mortal reach.

For items, Effective Mastery Level (EML) serves as the mastery equivalent. An item's EML determines:
- The power ceiling of its installed and innate effects
- Its position in the Construction Pool formula for spell-like abilities
- The tier of components it can support
- Its slot count (Tier = slots)

An item's EML grows through the Legacy system as the item accumulates history. A freshly forged item made from legendary materials begins with the properties its base material and mutation provide, but may have a low EML reflecting its lack of experiential depth. The materials set the ceiling; the item's lived history determines how much of that ceiling is currently expressed.

**EML Formula (Active Components Only):**
Highest Active CML + floor((Total Active Components − 1) ÷ 4)

Enabling Components (batteries, stabilizers, attunement matrices) do not count toward EML. Only Active Components count.

### PART VI: KEY VOCABULARY

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Prima Materia** | The framework governing the five planar engines and their interactions with matter, biology, technology, narrative, and entropy |
| **Isotope** | A specific planar expression defined by plane, phase composition, and interaction profile |
| **Formation Stage** | 1–5 rating of the intensity at which a material's planar expression crystallized; determines purity, crafting difficulty, and effect scaling |
| **Interaction Profile** | How a material's phase state behaves: Static (Solid), Cycling (Liquid), Projecting (Gas), Absorbing (Plasma) |
| **Alchemical Passport** | The complete reading of a material's planar identity from the alchemical periodic table — heritage, compatibility, Formation Stage, and Way participation |
| **Mutation** | A planar expression occurring in a material through exposure rather than installation; does not occupy a slot; self-powers its own effects |
| **Inset Component** | A removable magical or mechanical addition installed in an item's slot architecture; requires external battery power |
| **Additive** | A physical attachment carrying alchemical treatment that modifies the item's existing behavior without occupying an inset slot |
| **CML** | Component Mastery Level — the power rating of a single installed effect |
| **EML** | Effective Mastery Level — the overall power rating of a completed item, derived from its active components |
| **Catalyzed Diagonal** | A combination of competing phase states within the same plane, requiring sustained energy input to maintain |
| **Emulsion** | A combination of expressions from antithetical planes, requiring Forge catalyst to hold in tension |
| **Drip Recharge** | The system by which charges recover over time, anchored to the 10-minute base interval |
| **Mastery** | 1–10 rating of capability; 10 is peak mortal; the governing scale for both characters and items |
