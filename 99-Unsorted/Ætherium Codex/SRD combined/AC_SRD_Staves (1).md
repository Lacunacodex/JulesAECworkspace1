# THE ÆTHERIUM CODEX — System Reference Document
## Section: Staves — The Housing Architecture

*Alpha SRD Draft — Working Document*

---

### PREAMBLE

A staff is not a weapon that casts spells. It is a platform — an architectural housing built from a material whose quality determines how many systems it can support, how powerful those systems can be, and how they route to produce their effects. A wand is the same architecture at a smaller scale with a lower ceiling. The distinction between wand-grade and staff-grade is material quality, not purpose. Everything described in this section applies to both; the difference is capacity.

---

## PART I: MATERIAL GRADE AND CAPACITY

The item's base material Tier — derived from its physical properties as modified by any mutations — determines its architectural capacity. Tier equals slots. Tier caps the maximum EML the housing can support.

**Wand-Grade Materials (Tier 1-4)**

Materials at Tier 4 or below produce wand-grade housings. These items have a limited number of slots and a low EML ceiling. They are functional, effective, and fundamentally capped — no amount of work can make a Tier 3 material into a Tier 5 housing. The material simply cannot support the architecture.

A wand-grade item can hold 1-4 slots depending on Tier. This limits how many rotes, insets, and other components can be installed. Wand-grade items are the standard for single-purpose tools, consumer-grade magical equipment, and focused-application devices.

**Staff-Grade Materials (Tier 5-10)**

Materials at Tier 5 or above produce staff-grade housings. These items have the slot capacity and EML ceiling to support complex, multi-system architectures. A Tier 8 heartwood staff has 8 slots and can support up to EML 8. A Tier 10 material is the peak of mortal craftsmanship — 10 slots and EML 10 ceiling.

Staff-grade materials represent a significant investment in quality. A young practitioner might carry a Tier 8 staff with only one or two slots filled — a powerful housing with most of its potential unrealized. The staff grows with the wielder as new rotes and components are added over the course of a career. This is why staves are signature items — they represent both the material investment and the accumulated work of building out the architecture over time.

**The Tier-Capacity Table**

| Material Tier | Grade | Slots | EML Ceiling | Typical Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wand | 1 | 1 | Simple charms, minor focus objects |
| 2 | Wand | 2 | 2 | Hedge-wizard tools, basic wands |
| 3 | Wand | 3 | 3 | Standard wands, professional focuses |
| 4 | Wand | 4 | 4 | Quality wands, complex single-purpose devices |
| 5 | Staff | 5 | 5 | Journeyman staves, multi-purpose items |
| 6 | Staff | 6 | 6 | Professional staves, notable equipment |
| 7 | Staff | 7 | 7 | Expert-grade staves, significant items |
| 8 | Staff | 8 | 8 | Master-grade staves, regionally notable items |
| 9 | Staff | 9 | 9 | Exceptional staves, legendary potential |
| 10 | Staff | 10 | 10 | Peak mortal craftsmanship, nation-shaping potential |

**The Ceiling Versus Current Expression**

A Tier 8 staff with a single EML 2 rote is currently an EML 2 item. Its material Tier determines what it COULD support. Its current EML reflects what it actually contains. The wielder carries an item with vast unrealized potential — empty slots awaiting components, an EML ceiling far above its current expression.

This means material quality is an investment in the future. The crafter who sources a Tier 8 material for a beginning practitioner is building a platform that will serve for a career, not a disposable tool to be replaced at the next power threshold.

## PART II: INTERNAL ROTES AND EXPRESSIONS

A staff's base material — through its mutations and enchantments — can possess its own internal rotes. These are expressions that belong to the material itself, produced by the item's planar nature. They are not installed components. They are what the material does.

A heartwood staff with a V-01 World Tree mutation may innately produce healing or growth effects. An obsidian staff with a B-10 Magma heritage mutation may innately produce radiant heat or fire damage. These internal rotes are built through the spell sentence grammar and their parameters are determined by the material's physical properties feeding into the Construction Pool formula.

**Internal rotes:**
- Originate from the base material's mutations and enchantments
- Use the item's own EML when activated by triggering
- Generate their own charges through the drip recharge system based on Formation Stage and interaction profile
- Are permanent features of the item — they cannot be removed without alchemical decomposition of the mutation itself
- Count as Active Expressions toward the item's EML calculation
- Route through the item's internal architecture and can be modified by insets they pass through

## PART III: WAND CONFIGURATIONS

A wand — whether standalone or installed as an attachment in a staff — can be configured in three ways depending on what occupies its slots.

### Configuration 1: Rote Wand

The wand holds one rote as its primary function. This is the self-contained magical tool — point and activate. When triggered, the rote activates using the item's own EML, powered by the material's self-generated charges.

A non-practitioner can use a rote wand. The item does all the work. The wielder provides the trigger. This is the consumer-grade magical device — accessible, reliable, limited.

### Configuration 2: Conduit Wand

The wand holds no rotes. Its slots contain only insets — spell sentence modifiers that alter specific parameters. The wand is an empty channel waiting for input.

The caster feeds their own spell through the wand. The spell enters the material, passes through the insets, and exits modified. A conduit wand with a Chain inset and a Range inset takes the caster's fire damage rote and adds Chain targeting and extended range. The wand didn't produce the spell — it refined it.

This is the prism model. Light goes in, modified light comes out. The caster provides the Effect and Isotope (the substance of the spell). The wand provides consistent, repeatable modifications to the construction parameters.

A conduit wand is useless to a non-practitioner — there's nothing to trigger because there's no rote. It's a tool for skilled casters who want reliable parameter modification without spending Complexity Pool on Additives.

### Configuration 3: Hybrid Wand

The wand holds one rote AND one or more insets. The rote can be activated independently (using item EML), or the caster can channel their own spell through the wand and receive the inset modifications.

The rote and the insets coexist in the same housing. The rote is a self-contained fallback — always available, always reliable. The insets provide modification capability for the caster's own work. A fire mage carrying a hybrid wand has both a guaranteed fire bolt (the rote) and a Channel+Range modifier for their own fire compositions.

## PART IV: STAFF ARCHITECTURE — THE PLATFORM

A staff combines the base material's internal rotes with slots for wand-attachments, insets, and other components. This produces a multi-system platform where several independent magical subsystems coexist in a single housing.

### Slot Allocation

Every slot on the staff can hold one of:

**Wand-Attachment** — A wand installed as a component of the staff. Carries one rote. Has its own material properties. Generates its own charges from its own material. The wand is a sub-item with its own stat profile, but it occupies a slot on the parent staff.

**Inset** — A spell sentence modifier installed directly into the staff's architecture. Modifies rotes or channeled spells that pass through its position in the routing. Does not hold its own rote. Does not generate its own charges.

**Battery** — A dedicated power storage component. Sacrifices a slot that could hold a rote or modifier to provide additional charge capacity. Uses the battery material's own planar character and recharge rate. A meaningful trade-off — more power budget at the cost of fewer capabilities.

**Forge Link** — A Forge Interfacial component that connects two or more slots, allowing them to share charge pools. The shared pool is capped by the lower (weaker) expression of the linked components. Linking doesn't enhance — it shares. Useful when one component generates charges faster than it uses them and another component needs more than it generates.

### The EML Calculation for Staves

**EML = Highest Active Expression + floor((Total Active Expressions − 1) ÷ 4)**

Active Expressions include:
- The staff's own internal rotes (from mutations/enchantments)
- Each wand-attachment's rote
- Any other rote-bearing component

Active Expressions do NOT include:
- Insets (these modify, they don't produce independent effects)
- Batteries (these store, they don't produce effects)
- Forge Links (these connect, they don't produce effects)

**Example:** A Tier 8 staff with:
- 2 internal rotes (from mutations) at CML 4 and CML 3
- 3 wand-attachments at CML 4, CML 3, and CML 2
- 2 insets (Chain modifier, Range modifier)
- 1 Forge Link connecting the two internal rotes

Total Active Expressions: 5 (2 internal + 3 wands)
Highest CML: 4
EML = 4 + floor((5-1) ÷ 4) = 4 + 1 = **EML 5**

This fits within the Tier 8 ceiling comfortably. The staff has room to grow — three more slots available and EML ceiling of 8 not yet approached.

## PART V: THE ROUTING ARCHITECTURE

When a spell is activated or channeled through a staff, it follows a physical path through the item's architecture. The spell is modified ONLY by what it actually passes through — the base material, any attachments in the path, and any insets in the path. Components the spell doesn't route through have no effect on it.

This makes the staff's internal routing a deliberate crafting decision. The Forge Channeled lane builds the channels. The Forge Interfacial lane builds the junctions and gates. The crafter who designs the routing makes permanent choices that shape how the item functions.

### Routing Configurations

**Linear Routing**

Everything feeds through everything in sequence. A spell enters at one end, passes through the base material, through attachment A, through attachment B, through all insets in order, and exits.

All modifiers stack. The spell receives every modification the architecture provides. Maximum modification output — but no selectivity. Every casting gets the full treatment whether the caster wants it or not.

Linear routing is the simplest architecture. Novice crafters default to it. It's appropriate for items with a single intended purpose where all modifications always apply.

**Parallel Routing**

Each attachment and its associated insets form an independent channel. The spell enters the base material and the wielder directs it to one specific channel. Only that channel's modifiers apply.

Maximum selectivity — the wielder chooses which circuit processes each casting. But each individual path has fewer modifiers than a linear stack. This is the architecture for multi-purpose staves where different channels serve different functions.

**Selective Routing**

Switching architecture with junction points. Forge Interfacial components at specific positions allow the wielder to direct flow between paths, combine paths, or bypass certain components.

A selective routing staff might have a fire channel and an ice channel that can each be used independently, plus a junction that allows both to fire simultaneously through a shared modifier. The wielder has tactical options — fire alone, ice alone, or both at once through the shared path.

Selective routing is the most complex architecture and requires Journeyman or Master-level Forge work to construct. It's the architecture of legendary staves built to serve decades of diverse use.

### Routing and Internal Rotes

When the staff activates one of its own internal rotes (from its mutations), the expression originates in the base material and follows whatever routing path the architecture provides. If the internal channel passes through an inset, the inset modifies the internal rote.

This means clever architecture can enhance a staff's innate abilities through strategically placed insets — even though the insets were installed later, they modify the material's built-in expressions because the expressions physically route through them.

### Routing and Channeled Spells

When a caster channels their own spell through the staff, the spell enters the base material and follows the designated path. The base material's physical properties (hardness, conductivity, density, elasticity) modify the spell parameters through the property-to-spell-sentence mapping. Any insets and attachments in the path further modify the spell.

The caster uses their own stats (Mastery, Effect Tier, Isotope Tier) for the spell's base construction. The staff's material properties add their contributions on top. The result is a composite expression that neither the caster nor the staff could produce alone.

## PART VI: CHARGE POOLS AND POWER

### The Self-Generating Principle

Every rote-bearing component generates its own charges through the drip recharge system. The staff's base material generates charges for its internal rotes. Each wand-attachment generates charges from its own material for its own rote. Generation rate is determined by the component's Formation Stage and interaction profile.

Charge pools are isolated by default. The staff's internal pool doesn't feed the wand-attachments. Wand A's pool doesn't feed Wand B. Each subsystem is energetically independent.

### Forge Links

A Forge Link component can bridge two or more charge pools, allowing them to share. The shared pool is capped by the lower expression of the linked components — the link creates a lowest-common-denominator connection, not an amplification.

Linking is useful when one component generates surplus charges (high Formation Stage, fast drip rate, infrequent activation) and another component runs a deficit (low Formation Stage, slow drip rate, frequent activation). The surplus feeds the deficit through the link.

### Battery Slots

A crafter can sacrifice a regular slot to install a dedicated battery component. The battery has its own material with its own Formation Stage, interaction profile, and recharge rate. It provides an additional charge pool that can be linked to other components through Forge Links.

This is a meaningful trade-off — one fewer rote or modifier in exchange for greater power sustainability. High-EML staves with many active rotes often need one or more battery slots to sustain full operational tempo.

### Channeled Spell Costs

When a caster channels their own spell through the staff, the ÆP cost comes from the caster's own ÆP pool. The staff's charge pools are not consumed — the caster is providing the energy, the staff is providing the modification. The staff's charges are only consumed when the staff activates its own internal rotes or when a wand-attachment's rote is triggered.

## PART VII: ACTIVATION AND ACTION ECONOMY

### Triggering an Item Rote

Activating a rote from the staff's internal expressions or from a wand-attachment is a standard action (same as casting the equivalent rote yourself). The item uses its own EML for the effect's parameters. The wielder does not need to be a practitioner — they provide the trigger, the item provides everything else.

### Channeling Through the Item

Channeling your own spell through the staff uses whatever action the spell normally requires. The staff's modifications apply automatically as the spell routes through the architecture. The caster uses their own stats. The material properties add their contributions. No additional action cost for the modification — it's happening as part of the casting because the spell is physically passing through the modifying components.

### Multiple Activations

Each rote in a staff is independent. Activating Wand A's rote does not prevent activating Wand B's rote on the next round (provided both have charges). The staff is a library of available options, not a single-use system.

However, each activation is still one standard action. A staff with six loaded rotes gives the wielder six tactical options — not six simultaneous activations. The action economy limits throughput, not the architecture.

## PART VIII: STAVES AS CAREER ITEMS

The staff architecture is designed to grow alongside the wielder's career. A practitioner's staff is not disposable equipment replaced at each power threshold — it is a long-term investment that develops over years.

### Early Career

A beginning practitioner acquires a staff-grade material (Tier 5-6) and installs one or two rotes appropriate to their current skill. Most slots are empty. The staff's EML is low — perhaps 2 or 3. The material's Tier represents potential that far exceeds current expression.

### Mid Career

The practitioner adds wand-attachments as they develop new capabilities. Insets are installed to enhance existing rotes. The routing architecture becomes more complex as Forge work connects and directs the growing system. The staff's EML climbs as more active expressions are added.

### Mastery

A master's staff is a fully realized platform — most or all slots occupied, sophisticated selective routing, Forge Links managing charge distribution, internal rotes at high CML from decades of Legacy development. The staff's EML approaches its Tier ceiling. The item has a history, a reputation, a Social condition track reflecting its legend.

### Legacy Integration

The staff accumulates Ascension Points through the same Legacy system as any other permanent item. Its experiences shape its Palimpsest probability space. An Ascension Trait that develops on the staff reflects not just what the wielder did with it but what the staff itself has been through — every rote activated, every spell channeled, every battle survived.

The staff's internal rotes can deepen through Legacy — a mutation that expressed at CML 3 when the staff was young might develop to CML 5 as the item's experiential depth grows. The material quality (Formation Stage) still caps the expression, but within that cap, the rote's power can increase through use.

## PART IX: ITEMS BEYOND STAVES AND WANDS

The wand/staff architecture applies to any item that houses magical expressions — not just literal sticks. A sword with rotes and insets uses the same architecture. A suit of armor with defensive expressions and routing channels follows the same principles. A ring, an amulet, a crown, a ship's keel — any item with a material Tier and slots.

The terms "wand-grade" and "staff-grade" describe the architectural capacity of the material, not the physical form of the object. A Tier 8 longsword is staff-grade — it has 8 slots and can support complex multi-system architecture with sophisticated routing. A Tier 3 dagger is wand-grade — 3 slots maximum, limited architecture.

The form of the item determines how the routing physically works (a sword's channel runs from pommel through grip through guard through blade — different from a staff's end-to-end channel), but the principles are identical. Material quality determines capacity. Slots hold components. Routing determines modification. Charge pools are isolated unless linked. The architecture is universal.
