**PSYCHE SCHEMA**

System Reference --- Version 4

Document 1A --- Vertebra 1: Schema

*The profiling instrument of the Ætherium Codex unified architecture*

**WHAT THE SCHEMA IS**

The Psyche Schema is the profiling instrument at Vertebra 1 of the
Codex's seven-vertebra spine. It answers three questions about any
entity: who are they, how do they process events, and what do they seek.
Every subsequent vertebra reads the Schema's output. Pressure Points
(Vertebra 2) are extracted from Schema anomalies. Fronts (Vertebra 3)
are built from Schema-driven motivations. Clocks (Vertebra 4) tick at
rates determined by Schema-derived methodology. The Schema is not a
character sheet supplement. It is the foundation the entire architecture
builds from.

The Schema applies to everything that has internal complexity. Player
characters, NPCs, institutions, territories, and gods all have Schemas
or Schema-equivalents (see Document 1B: Profiling Companions Guide).
Everything uses the same profiling architecture because everything
interacts with the same spine. The rules do not change between scales.
The content changes. The instrument is universal.

**The Schema as Narrative Energy Source**

The Schema generates Fulcrum Points --- the metacurrency for spine
manipulation. Internal tensions (outlier scores, asymmetric splits
between paired dimensions, pattern breaks) are pressure points. Each
pressure point generates one Fulcrum Point per session. A flat, simple
profile generates minimal narrative energy. A complex, contradictory
profile generates enormous narrative energy. Character depth IS
mechanical power in the Codex. Not because deeper characters have higher
stats. Because their internal contradictions produce more Fulcrum
Points.

**The Schema as a Genuine Instrument**

The Psyche Schema is a measurement tool, not a personality quiz. It does
not sort characters into types. It maps the internal landscape of
values, tensions, and emotional architecture with enough resolution to
predict behavior under pressure and identify where the interesting story
lives. The scores are coordinates in a dimensional space defined by the
gods' portfolios. The gods are the axis labels. The character is the
point plotted in that space.

**THE THREE SECTIONS**

The Schema is organized into three sections, each capturing a different
category of data. The sections are not hierarchical. They are three
lenses on one identity, each revealing information the others cannot.

  **II. The Selûnae** 3 paired dimensions mapping the character's

Sections I and II are derivable from the character's philosophical
position and institutional role. A GM generating an NPC can produce
reasonable scores for these sections from a brief character concept.
Section III (Heart's Compass) requires knowledge of the character's
personal emotional life and is the section that demands the most
authorial investment. The GM shortcut (see Section 9) acknowledges this
asymmetry.

**Positional Divinity as Coordinate System**

The gods' portfolios are not flavor text. They are the dimensional axes
of the Schema's measurement space. Each score is the character's
coordinate on that god's axis. The full Schema plots the character in a
22-dimensional space (11 pairs × 2 aspects) anchored by divine
principles. This is the Positional Divinity system expressed as
character architecture --- the gods define the space, the character
occupies a position within it, and the position determines which divine
aspects resonate with the character's soul.

Veneration occurs through performing a role within a god's portfolio,
not through formal worship. A character with Kaelen 85 venerates Bellum
Hegemon's productive-conflict aspect every time they engage in
purposeful struggle. The character's scores are simultaneously a
personality profile AND a map of which divine aspects they embody
through their actions.

**Face Drift as Personal Clock**

The Selûnae scores are not static. They drift over the course of a
campaign as the character accumulates experience. The direction and rate
of drift IS the character's personal clock. A character drifting from
Maiden toward Wench is claiming more agency. A character drifting from
Crone toward Hag is accumulating burdens faster than they can integrate
them. A character whose Selûnae scores are stable has reached
equilibrium with their fate. A character whose scores are in rapid drift
is in crisis.

Face drift is the only Schema section that changes without explicit
player or GM intervention. The drift happens naturally as the Legacy
System (Vertebra 7) reads the character's accumulated choices and
adjusts the temporal orientation accordingly. The player does not update
their Selûnae scores --- the GM updates them based on the pattern the
Legacy System reveals.

**THE COMPANION ELEMENTS**

The Schema's three sections profile the character's internal landscape.
Five companion elements provide the external context that shapes how the
internal landscape expresses.

**College Affiliation**

Which college shaped the character determines their methodology, their
persistent test, their failure state, and their operational vocabulary.
Two characters with identical Schema scores but different college
affiliations produce completely different fronts because the college
determines HOW the profile expresses. The Schema says what you value.
The college says how you pursue it.

College affiliation also determines the character's planar alignment
(which of the five planes their methodology channels), their operational
tempo (how quickly they act), and their institutional vocabulary (how
they describe what they do). See the College Initiations Canon for the
full initiation, persistent test, and failure state architecture of each
college.

**Archetype**

The mechanical chassis the profile operates through. The Schema is the
personality. The Archetype is the mechanical toolkit. A high-Kaelen
Wildpath character playing through a martial Archetype produces a
different set of mechanical expressions than the same Schema through a
caster Archetype. The Archetype constrains which mechanical expressions
of the Schema are available at Vertebra 6 (Adventures / Observation
Collapse).

**Legacy System**

The backward-facing identity that accumulates at Vertebra 7 (Feedback
Loop). At Vertebra 1, the Legacy is the character's starting state ---
what the Palimpsest has already recorded about this soul. The Legacy
system does not read forward from milestones. It reads BACKWARD from
accumulated pattern. The character's identity at session 1 is the Legacy
reading everything that came before. Over a campaign, the Legacy
accumulates new pattern from the character's choices, which in turn
modifies how the Schema is read --- not the scores themselves, but their
weight and significance.

**Soul Stone**

The physical container of the Schema data. In-setting, the soul stone
carries the god-name weights, aspect values, and full experiential
record. The Chirurgeons' query reads it. The Legacy system is stored in
it. The soul stone is the MEDIUM in which the Schema exists. This
matters because it means the Schema is a THING in the world --- it can
be read, queried, stolen, damaged, or transferred. The profile has a
physical instantiation that can be interacted with through the game's
mechanics.

**Positional Divinity**

The Schema's coordinate system (described in Section I) is
simultaneously a map of which divine aspects the character embodies. The
character's scores determine which gods' faces resonate with their soul,
which in turn determines which divine portfolio the character's actions
support. This is not worship. It is structural resonance. The character
venerates a god by being the kind of person whose actions fall within
that god's portfolio.

**THE ACTIVE PROPERTIES**

Three properties transform the Schema from a static profile into a
generative instrument that reaches into the spine and shapes play.

**Goffman: The Two-Faced Profile**

Every profiled entity has a front stage (what they present to the world)
and a back stage (what they actually think, feel, and plan). The gap
between front stage and back stage IS data. The Schema's surface scores
are the front stage. The outliers, asymmetric splits, and pattern breaks
are where the back stage leaks through.

At Vertebra 2 (Pressure Points), the Goffman gap is a primary source of
extractable pressure. When you identify that an NPC's presented behavior
contradicts their Schema scores, that contradiction IS a pressure point.
The NPC Assessment Card's Contradictions Log is literally a Goffman gap
tracker --- recording the observable discrepancies between what the NPC
presents and what their profile predicts.

For player characters, the Goffman gap is chosen by the player. They
decide what their character presents versus what they actually feel. For
GM-generated NPCs, the gap is derived from the Schema --- the front
stage is what the NPC's institutional role demands, and the back stage
is what the Schema scores reveal about who they actually are underneath
the role.

**Beliefs and Instincts**

Beliefs are Schema elements stated as active convictions: "I will
protect the innocent." "The law exists to serve power, not justice." "No
one should die alone." Each belief is anchored to one or more Schema
dimensions and represents the character's commitment to acting on that
dimension's values. Beliefs CREATE mandatory engagement --- certain
seeds at Vertebra 5 (Symptoms) automatically trigger observation
collapse at Vertebra 6 (Adventures) because the participant's belief
demands response. The character CANNOT walk away from situations that
challenge their beliefs. This is the bonding mechanism between Vertebra
1 (who you are) and Vertebra 6 (how you engage).

Instincts are automatic behavioral rules: "I always check for exits." "I
never turn my back on someone I don't trust." "I share food before
eating." Instincts are Seeds that collapse automatically without
deliberation. They produce consistent observable behavior that other
characters and the audience can track. Instincts are the leitmotifs of
the expression layer --- the recurring behaviors that encode the
character's identity in the group's collective memory.

Players write 2-3 beliefs and 2-3 instincts during character creation.
Beliefs can change through play when the character's convictions are
tested and found wanting. Instincts are deeper --- they change only
through transformative experiences (initiations, traumatic events,
Legacy system crystallization).

**Narrative Identity Type (McAdams)**

How the character processes events --- the interpretive template they
apply to experience. Four types:

  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  **Redemption**      Bad events are processed as setups for eventual
                      positive outcomes. The character finds meaning in
                      suffering by believing it leads somewhere better.
                      The suffering was worth it because of what came
                      after.

  **Contamination**   Good events are processed as precursors to loss.
                      The character anticipates that positive experiences
                      will be corrupted or destroyed. Joy is temporary
                      because pain follows.

  **Agency**          Events are processed through the lens of personal
                      control. The character believes their choices
                      determine outcomes. Success is earned. Failure is
                      the character's responsibility.

  **Communion**       Events are processed through relationships. The
                      character's experience is defined by connection to
                      others. Success is shared. Loss is relational.
  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------

The Narrative Identity type is not a Schema score. It is a processing
template that determines how the character INTERPRETS the events the
spine produces. The same clock advancement produces different character
reactions depending on which type processes it. A Redemption character
whose clock crosses the intervention threshold sees it as the darkest
moment before the turning point. A Contamination character sees it as
confirmation that the positive trajectory was always going to fail.

The type is derived from the character's backstory and their current
Heart's Compass face distribution. Characters with predominantly
Balanced faces tend toward Redemption or Agency. Characters with
predominantly Trauma faces tend toward Contamination. Characters with
high Silas/Aethel/Zeniel scores tend toward Communion. The type can
shift through play as the character's Heart's Compass faces change.

**FULCRUM POINT GENERATION**

The Schema generates Fulcrum Points from its internal tensions. The
generation mechanic connects Vertebra 1 (Schema) to Vertebra 4 (Clocks)
where Fulcrum Points are spent. See Document 4B (Fulcrum Points System)
for the complete spend mechanic, distance model, and bidirectional
response architecture.

**Pressure Point Identification**

Three types of pressure points generate Fulcrum Points:

**Outlier Scores:** Any single aspect score above 80 or below 20 in any
section. Extreme scores represent convictions so deep they are
load-bearing --- the character's identity would collapse without them.
Convictions this deep create pressure because reality constantly tests
them. Each outlier generates 1 FP.

**Asymmetric Splits:** Any paired dimension where the gap between the
two aspects exceeds 50 points. A 75-point gap between Veritas 90 and
Gnosis 15 is not a preference --- it is an emotional wound disguised as
a philosophical position. The world will inevitably present situations
where the suppressed aspect is the correct response, and the character
cannot access it. Each asymmetric split generates 1 FP.

**Pattern Breaks:** Scores that contradict the expected profile for the
character's college, role, or presented identity. An immortal warden
with a moderate-high Veyra (Unrequited) score breaks the pattern.
Pattern breaks indicate hidden pressures the character themselves may
not be aware of. Each pattern break generates 1 FP.

**Generation Formula**

Fulcrum Points per session = (count of outlier scores) + (count of
asymmetric splits) + (count of pattern breaks).

A Tier 0 Everyman with a mostly centered Schema might generate 1-2 FP. A
headmaster with massive outliers, enormous splits, and multiple pattern
breaks might generate 12-15 FP. The system rewards character complexity
with narrative energy. This is the same formula applied to NPCs,
institutions, territories, and gods --- the GM's Fulcrum Point budget
for any entity is generated by the same instrument from the same source.

**THE PLAYER QUESTIONNAIRE**

For player characters, the Schema is generated through a structured
intake process. The player answers questions about their character's
values, history, and emotional life. The GM derives scores from the
answers. The derivation uses the questions as anchors --- the specific
answers map to specific dimensional ranges.

**Intake Sections**

  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  **1. The Concept**  Name, alias, core concept in one sentence. What is
                      this character about?

  **2. The            Questions mapping to each Core Philosophy pair.
  Philosophy**        "When rules conflict with justice, which do you
                      follow?" (Nomos: Themis vs. Lex). "Is struggle the
                      point of living or the obstacle to living?" (Moros:
                      Silas vs. Dolor). One question per divine pair.

  **3. The Timeline** Questions mapping to the Selûnae pairs. "What came
                      to you without seeking it?" (Maiden). "What did you
                      take by force?" (Wench). "What bond has lasted
                      longest?" (Mother). "What relationship did you
                      leave behind?" (Strumpet). "What lesson cost you
                      the most?" (Crone). "What consequence do you still
                      carry?" (Hag).

  **4. The Heart**    Questions mapping to the Heart's Compass
                      dimensions. "Who do you love and how?" (Isolde).
                      "Who would you die for?" (Eselle). "When did you
                      last laugh so hard you couldn't breathe?" (Corin).
                      "What obligation do you carry that you didn't
                      choose?" (Constantine). For each: which face is
                      active (Balanced, Unrestrained, Trauma)?

  **5. The Toolkit**  Defining moment, signature skill, greatest
                      weakness, method of operation. These are not scored
                      --- they contextualize the scores. The defining
                      moment often reveals the Heart's Compass face
                      transitions. The weakness often maps to the
                      lowest-scoring dimension.
  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------

The GM reviews the answers and assigns scores using the 10-band overlay
as a guide. A strong, specific answer about a dimension suggests a high
score (61-100). A vague or uncertain answer suggests a mid-range score
(41-60). A deflection or avoidance suggests either a very low score (the
dimension is absent) or a Trauma face (the dimension is present but
painful). The questionnaire produces an initial Schema that the first
few sessions of play will refine.

After the questionnaire, the player writes 2-3 Beliefs (active
convictions anchored to Schema dimensions) and 2-3 Instincts (automatic
behavioral rules). These are the Schema's active components that reach
into the spine during play.

**THE GM SHORTCUT**

The full questionnaire is player-facing. GMs generating NPCs need a
compressed procedure that produces a functional Schema from minimal
inputs. The shortcut acknowledges that GM-generated scores are authored
estimates rather than derived measurements --- the precision is
approximate, but the diagnostic function (pressure point identification)
still works.

**The Five-Question NPC Generator**

  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  **1. College and    Which college trained them? What role do they
  Position**          occupy? These two facts constrain the Core
                      Philosophy scores --- a Stormpeak officer will have
                      high Bellum Hegemon scores, and the specific
                      balance between Kaelen and Bellax depends on
                      whether they're trending toward mastery or failure.

  **2. The Want**     What do they want right now? The want maps to the
                      highest Core Philosophy scores. A character who
                      wants justice has high Nomos. A character who wants
                      knowledge has high Vestus. The specific face
                      (Themis vs. Lex, Veritas vs. Gnosis) reveals their
                      methodology.

  **3. The Fear**     What are they afraid of? The fear maps to the
                      lowest scores or the Trauma faces in the Heart's
                      Compass. A character afraid of abandonment has
                      Silas or Aethel in Trauma face. A character afraid
                      of losing control has Rhapsodia tension.

  **4. The            What's one thing about them that doesn't match
  Contradiction**     their role? This is the deliberate pattern break
                      --- the score that generates the mystery front. The
                      military commander with a secret artistic passion.
                      The healer who enjoys the power that proximity to

  **5. Clock          Where are they on their college's personal clock?
  Position**          Early (still learning), middle (competent but

From these five answers, the GM assigns approximate scores to the 3-5
most relevant Core Philosophy dimensions, the dominant Selûnae pair, and
the 2-3 most active Heart's Compass dimensions. Everything else is left
at 50 (neutral). The pressure points are identifiable from the extremes
and the contradiction. The NPC is functional for engine derivation.

Section III (Heart's Compass) is the section that requires the most
creative authorship for GM-generated NPCs. The engine acknowledges this:
Sections I and II are derivable from position and philosophy. Section
III requires the GM to make character choices that the engine cannot
generate. The five-question shortcut minimizes this burden by asking for
only one Heart's Compass data point (the fear) and one deliberate
pattern break (the contradiction).

**THE COMPOSITE SCORES**

Two composite scores sit outside the three sections. They are derived
from the full Schema but are not dimensions themselves --- they are
emergent properties of the overall profile.

**Aíon (Cosmic Stability)**

A measure of how well the character's identity integrates into the
cosmic architecture. High Aíon stability indicates a profile that is
coherent, internally consistent, and aligned with the setting's
structural logic. The character makes sense within the world. Their
actions are predictable from their profile. The spine models them
accurately.

The Aíon score is relevant to the Smoothing Effect --- characters with
high cosmic stability encounter less resistance from the setting's
coherence-maintenance architecture. Their actions are already within the
parameters the Smoothing Effect permits. Characters with low cosmic
stability encounter more friction because their existence is itself a
discontinuity the Smoothing Effect wants to dampen.

**The Lacuna (Cosmic Instability)**

A measure of how much the character's identity disrupts the cosmic
architecture. High Lacuna instability indicates a profile that contains
contradictions the setting's structural logic cannot fully resolve. The
character is a discontinuity --- a point where the Smoothing Effect
cannot fully suppress the gap between the two merged settings, or
between what the character's profile predicts and what they actually do.

The relationship between Aíon and Lacuna is not inversely proportional.
A character can have high stability AND high instability simultaneously
--- they are deeply integrated into the cosmic architecture AND they are
a source of disruption within it. These characters are the most
narratively interesting because they are simultaneously essential to the
system and threatening to it.

The derivation formula for both composite scores is pending calibration
and will be finalized in the game system's mathematical framework. The
conceptual definitions are established. The specific numerical
derivation requires playtesting.

**STORY STACK VALIDATION**

Every completed Schema should be tested against the Story Stack's four
layers to ensure the profile is rich enough to sustain play across all
dimensions of the game.

  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  **Fantasy**         Who does the player want to BE through this
                      character? The Schema should support the player's
                      fantasy --- the version of themselves they want to
                      explore. If the player wants to be a cunning
                      strategist, the Schema should have high Kanon and
                      Vestus scores. If the fantasy is a passionate
                      defender, the Schema needs high Bellum Hegemon and
                      Constantine.

  **Actions**         What does the player want to DO? The Schema's
                      active components (Beliefs and Instincts) should
                      generate engagement opportunities. If there are no
                      Beliefs that force interaction with the world, the
                      character will be passive. Every Schema should
                      produce at least 2-3 situations per session where a
                      Belief demands response.

  **Economy**         How does the character interact with the game's
                      systems? The 10-band overlay should produce a
                      distinctive mechanical signature. Characters whose
                      dice pools are all d8 (everything at 41-60) are
                      mechanically bland. Characters with d12s and d4s
                      (strong outliers) have mechanical texture that
                      creates meaningful choices.

  **World**           How does the character relate to the setting?
                      College affiliation, Positional Divinity resonance,
                      and the Selûnae's temporal orientation should
                      connect the character to the setting's cosmology. A
                      character with no connection to any college, no
                      divine resonance, and no temporal orientation is
                      floating free of the world rather than embedded in
                      it.
  ------------------- ---------------------------------------------------

If a Schema fails one or more layers, the solution is not to add scores.
It is to add specificity. A character who fails the Actions layer needs
sharper Beliefs. A character who fails the World layer needs clearer
college affiliation or divine resonance. A character who fails the
Fantasy layer needs a conversation between the player and the GM about
what the player actually wants to experience.

**EXPRESSION GUIDANCE: PERSISTENCE ENCODING**

The Schema's strongest expression bond is Persistence Encoding --- the
techniques that anchor a profiled entity in the group's collective
memory across sessions. Without encoding, the profile is forgotten
between encounters. With encoding, the character becomes a recognizable
presence that the group tracks and anticipates.

**Leitmotifs**

A recurring verbal phrase, physical action, or behavioral pattern
associated with the character. The phrase or action appears whenever the
character is present and the associated Schema dimension is active. Over
time, the leitmotif carries the character's full emotional weight in a
few words or gestures.

**Design principle:** One per character maximum. More than one dilutes
the signal. The leitmotif should be derived from the character's highest
or most distinctive Schema dimension.

**Behavioral Signatures**

The NPC personality technique: one behavior (repeated physical action
when not addressed), one opinion (editorial perspective nobody asked
for), one refusal (what they won't do). Each maps to a Schema dimension.
The behavior maps to the dominant Core Philosophy face. The opinion maps
to the strongest Belief. The refusal maps to the lowest score or a
Trauma-face dimension.

**Third-Person Narration**

Report instead of perform. "The Docent goes quiet and then says the
symbol is familiar but won't elaborate" communicates mystery without
performing it. The emotional cue is the narrator's contribution. The
audience's imagination provides the performance. Each participant
generates a different character in their imagination from the same cue.
The underspecification is a feature --- Schema Theory (Bartlett) says
the audience's schema fills the gap with personally meaningful content.

**KNOWN LIMITATIONS AND DIAGNOSTIC FINDINGS**

From proof-of-concept testing (Kyril full derivation, Colonel reverse
derivation):

  -------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  **False Precision**  GM-authored NPC scores feel authoritative but are
                       actually estimates. Why 85 and not 80? There is no
                       derivation formula for GM-generated scores. The
                       precision is approximate. The diagnostic function
                       (pressure point identification) still works because
                       it reads for RELATIVE extremes, not absolute
                       values. An outlier is an outlier whether the score
                       is 82 or 88.

  **Heart's Compass    Sections I-II are derivable from position and
  Authorship**         philosophy. Section III requires the GM to make
                       character choices the engine cannot generate. The
                       GM shortcut minimizes this burden but does not
                       eliminate it.

  **External Pressure  The engine's derivation handles NPC-internal
  Gap**                pressure (what the Schema reveals) but not
                       NPC-external pressure (what the environment does TO
                       the person regardless of Schema). An additional
                       derivation step is needed: "What pressures does
                       this POSITION generate independent of who occupies
                       it?"

  **Creative           The mapping from pressure point to front requires
  Interpretation       creative interpretation informed by setting
  Step**               knowledge. This step needs to be named explicitly
                       in the procedure rather than hidden.

  **Clock              Schema derivation produces fronts with potential
  Parameterization**   clocks, not parameterized clocks. Specific clock
                       values (segments, thresholds, terminal states) need
                       play context. The Schema is a prep tool. The clock
                       template is a play tool.
  -------------------- ---------------------------------------------------

These are edge-case limitations, not architecture failures. The core
pipeline --- Schema to pressure points to fronts --- holds up. The gaps
are at the inputs (how to generate scores without a full questionnaire)
and the outputs (how to instantiate clocks when the NPC enters active
play). The middle of the pipeline works.
