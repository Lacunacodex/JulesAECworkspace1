**FACTION TURN PROCEDURES**

How the World Moves Between Sessions

Document 3C --- Vertebra 3: Fronts (Companion)

*The movement mechanism that makes the setting a living system*

**WHAT A FACTION TURN IS**

Between sessions, the world moves. Fronts pursue their goals. Clocks
advance. Relationships shift. The party returns to a setting that is
different from the one they left --- not because the GM authored a
change, but because the factions took their turns.

A faction turn is one action taken by one front between sessions. The
action advances or retards a clock, creates or modifies an edge on the
relationship map, produces observable symptoms the party will encounter,
and potentially triggers consequence propagation to connected fronts.
The faction turn is the engine that makes the Default Timeline advance
toward its terminal states when the party is not intervening.

The purpose is not to simulate a political strategy game between
sessions. The purpose is to produce a world that feels alive --- one
where the party's sense that things happened while they were away is
grounded in actual events that the GM can trace, explain, and connect to
the network of fronts and relationships. The world is not static between
sessions. The faction turn is the minimum mechanism that prevents
stasis.

**THE FOUR ACTION TYPES**

Every faction turn is one of four action types. The type determines what
kind of clock the action advances, what kind of symptom it produces, and
how the consequence propagates through the relationship map.

**Expand**

The front extends its reach. It acquires new territory, recruits new
members, establishes new operations, or increases its resource base.
Expansion is the offensive action that grows the front's footprint in
the setting.

**Clock effect:** Advances clocks tracking the front's geographic or
institutional growth. Symptoms: new personnel appearing in areas the
front didn't previously operate, new infrastructure under construction,
recruitment efforts visible to the public.

**Map effect:** Increases tension on edges with rival and enemy fronts
whose territory or resources are being encroached upon. May increase
trust on edges with ally fronts who benefit from the expansion. May
create new edges where the expanding front enters a new node's sphere of
influence.

**Defend**

The front consolidates its position. It fortifies existing operations,
secures resources, hardens against anticipated threats, or conducts
internal maintenance. Defense is the reactive action that protects what
the front already has.

**Clock effect:** Retards clocks tracking threats to the front. Advances
clocks tracking the front's internal stability. Symptoms: increased
security presence, access restrictions, fortification activity, internal
audits.

**Map effect:** Decreases tension on edges with the front's allies
(reassurance). May increase tension with rivals who interpret defensive
posture as preparation for aggression. No new edges created.

**Attack**

The front actively undermines, disrupts, or destroys a specific target.
Attack is the aggressive action that directly opposes another front's
interests. Not limited to military action --- economic sabotage,
political maneuvering, social manipulation, and intelligence operations
are all attack actions.

**Clock effect:** Advances clocks tracking the target front's
degradation or threat level. May retard the target's operational clocks.
Symptoms: disruptions to the target's operations, damage to their
assets, interference with their personnel, visible conflict between the
two fronts.

**Map effect:** Dramatically increases tension on the edge with the
target. May trigger a state change (Neutral → Rival, Rival → Enemy).
Increases tension on edges with the target's allies. May decrease
tension on edges with the target's enemies (the enemy of my enemy).

**Influence**

The front shapes perception, builds alliances, or maneuvers politically
without direct confrontation. Influence is the indirect action that
modifies relationships rather than resources. The most subtle action
type and the one whose consequences are hardest for the party to trace.

**Clock effect:** Advances clocks tracking social or political shifts.
May advance clocks on other fronts whose behavior is being shaped.
Symptoms: shifts in public opinion, changed NPC attitudes, new alliances
or partnerships, subtle behavioral changes in connected NPCs.

**Map effect:** Directly modifies edge properties. May increase trust
between the front and its targets. May increase or decrease tension on
edges the front is trying to manipulate. May create new debt
relationships. The influence action is the primary mechanism for
modifying the map's wiring.

**THE RESOLUTION PROCEDURE**

How the GM determines whether a faction turn action succeeds, partially
succeeds, or fails.

**The Three Factors**

Every faction turn resolution considers three factors:

**1. Asset Match:** The front's available resources for this action
type. A Stormpeak front with depleted military assets attempting an
Expand action has fewer resources than one at full strength. The front's
Asset field (Document 3A, Field 4) determines what the front can deploy.
Depleted assets reduce the action's effectiveness.

**2. Opposition:** Active opposition from connected fronts. If the
target of an Attack action has taken a Defend action this turn, the
defense opposes the attack. If a front is Expanding into territory a
rival front is also Expanding into, the two actions oppose each other.
Unopposed actions succeed more completely than opposed ones.

**3. Smoothing Resistance:** The Smoothing Effect's resistance to
change. Small actions in stable territories encounter minimal
resistance. Large actions in stable territories or any action in
unstable territories encounter more resistance. The territory's Zoetrope
position stability and the action's magnitude determine how much
Smoothing dampening applies.

**Resolution Method**

The GM has two options for resolution:

**Option A: Adjudicated Resolution**

The GM considers the three factors and determines the outcome through
judgment. This is the default for most campaigns. The GM asks: does the
front have the resources, is anyone opposing them, and how much does the
setting resist this change? The answer produces one of three outcomes:

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Full Success**   The action achieves its intended effect. The
                     relevant clock advances one segment. Observable
                     symptoms are produced. Map effects apply at full
                     strength.

  **Partial          The action achieves part of its intended effect, or
  Success**          achieves the full effect with an unintended side
                     effect. The clock advances half a segment (rounded
                     down) or advances fully but produces a complication
                     on a connected edge. The most common outcome --- the
                     world resists clean resolution.

  **Failure**        The action does not achieve its intended effect. The
                     clock does not advance. The attempt itself may
                     produce symptoms (failed operations are visible) and
                     may increase tension on edges with fronts who
                     detected the attempt.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

**Option B: Mechanical Resolution**

For GMs who prefer dice-driven outcomes, a simple mechanical procedure:

The front rolls against a Target Number (TN) set by the GM based on the
three factors. Strong assets and weak opposition produce a low TN
(easy). Depleted assets, strong opposition, and high Smoothing
resistance produce a high TN (difficult).

The roll uses the front's driving NPC's relevant Schema dimension die
(from the 10-band overlay, Document 1A). An Expand action uses the
dimension most relevant to the expansion methodology. A Stormpeak
military expansion rolls the Kaelen die. A Unity social expansion rolls
the Agora Architect die. A Chronosynclastic intelligence expansion rolls
the Vestus Gnosis die.

Meet or exceed the TN: full success. Miss by 1-3: partial success. Miss
by 4+: failure. The Schema's die type IS the front's competence level
for this type of action. A front driven by an NPC with a d12 in the
relevant dimension is significantly more effective than one driven by an
NPC with a d6.

The full procedure takes fifteen to thirty minutes depending on the
number of active fronts. Most campaigns have three to six active fronts
at any time. Each front takes one action per turn. The resolution is
quick because the GM is making judgment calls informed by the map's
properties, not running a complex simulation.

**SCALING THE FACTION TURN**

Not every front needs to take an action every between-session turn. The
GM manages the number of active turns based on campaign complexity and
available prep time.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Early Campaign** 1-3 active fronts taking turns. The party has
                     limited awareness of the setting's power structure.
                     Only the fronts directly relevant to their current
                     adventure are active. The world moves subtly.

  **Mid Campaign**   3-6 active fronts. The party has established
                     relationships with multiple NPCs and institutions.
                     More fronts are visible. The world moves noticeably
                     --- the party returns to find things changed.

  **Late Campaign**  6-10 active fronts. The party is embedded in the
                     network. Multiple fronts interact, producing
                     cascading consequences. The world moves dramatically
                     --- major shifts happen between sessions.

  **Climax**         All active fronts converge. The faction turn
                     procedure runs at maximum density. Multiple opposed
                     actions resolve simultaneously. The setting's
                     pressure cooker regions (Document 3B) erupt. This is
                     the phase where the Default Timeline and the party's
                     actual timeline diverge most dramatically.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

The GM should never run more faction turns than they can resolve
meaningfully in available prep time. A rushed faction turn produces
incoherent consequences. A thoughtful faction turn for three fronts
produces more useful play material than a hasty faction turn for ten.
Quality over quantity. If the GM has twenty minutes, run three fronts
thoroughly. If they have an hour, run six.

**THE PLAYER TRIGGER**

Faction turns do not only happen between sessions. Player actions during
a session can trigger immediate faction responses.

When the party takes an action that directly affects a front ---
disrupting an operation, killing a key NPC, exposing a secret, making an
alliance --- the affected front may take an immediate response action.
This is not a full faction turn. It is a reactive action triggered by
the party's intervention.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Disrupted        The front Defends: secures remaining assets, locks
  Operation**        down compromised operations, assesses damage.
                     Symptoms appear within the same session or the next.

  **Key NPC          The front either Defends (consolidates around the
  Removed**          next-in-command) or Attacks (retaliates against
                     whoever removed the NPC). The response depends on
                     the institutional culture. Stormpeak retaliates.
                     Deepguard consolidates. Unity manages the narrative.

  **Secret Exposed** The front Influences: damage control,
                     counter-narrative deployment, witness management.
                     The Chiaroscuro methodology is particularly active
                     here. Unity's methodology overlaps.

  **Alliance         Connected rival fronts may Attack (preemptive
  Formed**           disruption of the new alliance) or Influence
                     (attempting to dissolve or co-opt the alliance). The
                     response propagates through the relationship map
                     along high-tension edges.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

Player-triggered reactions happen within the session's normal flow. The
GM processes them in real time using the same resolution procedure
(three factors, adjudicated outcome). The triggered reactions produce
immediate symptoms the party encounters during the same session. This
creates the feeling that the setting is responsive --- the party's
actions produce visible reactions from the power structure.

**THE ZOETROPE COUPLING**

Accumulated faction turn resolutions within a territory push the
Zoetrope position toward transition. This is the mechanism by which
individual and institutional actions aggregate into civilizational
change.

**How Accumulation Works**

Every resolved faction turn action within a territory adds weight to the
Zoetrope's directional pressure. The weight is determined by the college
methodology behind the action. When enough actions from the same
methodological family accumulate, the Zoetrope position begins to slide
toward the position associated with that methodology.

A territory where Deepguard fronts dominate the faction turn landscape
accumulates abjuration-methodology weight. If sustained, the territory
drifts toward Zoetrope positions associated with institutional
governance, legal infrastructure, and engineered stability. A territory
where Wildpath fronts dominate accumulates transmutation-methodology
weight, drifting toward positions associated with ecological integration
and biological governance.

**The Threshold**

Zoetrope positions do not change from individual faction turns. They
change when the accumulated weight of faction turn resolutions overcomes
the Smoothing Effect's resistance at the civilizational scale. This
requires sustained, consistent pressure over multiple campaign arcs ---
not sessions, but arcs. The Zoetrope operates at the longest timescale
in the system.

The GM tracks Zoetrope pressure qualitatively: no pressure, building
pressure, critical pressure. When critical pressure is reached, the next
arc's events will include the Zoetrope transition as a background event.
The transition itself is the source of new territorial fronts --- the
old power structure giving way to the new, producing new institutional
conflicts, new resource competitions, and new adventure material.

**The Party's Role**

The party does not control Zoetrope transitions. But their actions
influence which faction turns succeed and which fail. A party that
consistently supports Stormpeak operations and disrupts Wildpath
operations is, over time, adding military-methodology weight to their
territory's Zoetrope pressure. They may not realize they are shaping
civilizational drift. They are making choices about individual fronts.
The aggregate consequence emerges from the pattern of choices read
backward --- the Selûnae principle applied at the setting scale.

**FACTION TURNS AND FULCRUM POINTS**

Institutional FP (Document 1B) fuel faction turn actions. The
institutional FP pool generated by the institution's Schema tensions
represents the narrative energy available for faction-level operations.

**NPC FP Spends:** The driving NPC may spend personal FP during faction
turn resolution to improve outcomes. A Distance 0 spend adds to the
resolution roll or tips an adjudicated judgment. A Distance 1-2 spend
shapes the consequence propagation --- directing which edges carry the
effect, or damping an unintended side effect. The NPC's Mastery
determines how far they can reach.

**Institutional FP Spends:** The institution's FP pool funds ambitious,
large-scale, or surprising faction turn actions. A front taking an
action that exceeds its normal operational scope (a Deepguard front
attempting a social influence operation rather than a legal one)
requires institutional FP to cover the methodology stretch. Operating
outside your college's native methodology is possible but costs
narrative energy.

**Deferred Consequences:** Each FP spent during a faction turn produces
deferred consequences through the normal spine propagation. The skipped
vertebrae process the discontinuity. The GM notes which vertebrae were
skipped and generates side effects between sessions. This means
ambitious faction turn actions --- the ones that produce the most
dramatic outcomes --- also produce the most unintended consequences. The
system self-balances.

**PRACTICAL EXAMPLE: ONE BETWEEN-SESSION TURN**

Three active fronts. The GM runs the ten-step procedure.

**Step 1: List Active Fronts**

Front A: Kyril's Vestus Containment (Mortis Ævum). Front B: Vestus's
Archive Probe (Chronosynclastic-adjacent, run by Eternal Scholars).
Front C: Sovereign Exchange trade route expansion into the City of
Folly.

**Step 2: Select Actions**

Front A: Defend (Kyril hardens archive access protocols). Front B:
Attack (Eternal Scholars attempt a covert information extraction). Front
C: Expand (Exchange establishes a new trade office in the City of
Folly).

**Step 3: Check Opposition**

Front A's Defend directly opposes Front B's Attack. Front C's Expand is
unopposed --- nobody is actively preventing the Exchange from opening a
trade office.

**Steps 4-5: Resolve and Advance**

Front A vs. B: Kyril's Defend is well-resourced (archive security is
Mortis Ævum's core competency). The Eternal Scholars' Attack is opposed.
Result: partial success for the Scholars. They didn't extract the target
information, but they identified a vulnerability in the archive's outer
layer. The probe clock advances half a segment. Kyril's containment
clock does not advance (defense held). But Kyril's attention was
diverted --- note for Step 9.

Front C: Unopposed Expand with strong assets (the Exchange has money).
Full success. The trade office is established. The expansion clock
advances one segment. Observable symptoms: new Exchange personnel
arriving in the City, a building being renovated, trade goods appearing
in the market that weren't available before.

**Steps 6-7: Symptoms and Propagation**

Front B's partial success: the Scholars discovered a vulnerability. This
propagates along the Scholars→Vestus edge (high trust) --- Vestus learns
of the vulnerability accurately. Tension on the Kyril→Vestus edge
increases (Kyril detected the probe attempt). The party may encounter
the symptom: Kyril's archive staff implementing new, unexplained
security procedures.

Front C's success: The Exchange's presence propagates along their edges.
The Mortis Ævum→Exchange edge carries the consequence as data (neutral
state). Kyril notes the Exchange's arrival but does not act. The
Political Leverage Problem front's clock ticks --- another institution
establishing a foothold near the college. The party may encounter the
symptom: new merchants in town with unfamiliar goods, a building being
renovated, Exchange banners appearing.

**Steps 8-10: Update, Check, Flag**

Map updates: Kyril→Vestus tension increased to High (approaching state
change). New edge created: Exchange→City of Folly (trust medium, no
debt, tension low, state neutral). NPC clock check: Kyril's attention
was diverted by the Scholars' probe --- his Institutional Maintenance
front may show strain next session. Flag for next session: the new
security procedures (Front B symptom) and the Exchange's arrival (Front
C symptom) as opening hooks.
