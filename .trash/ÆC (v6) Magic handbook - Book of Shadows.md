The System of Magical Attainment: Temporary & Permanent Boosts

In Tessera, a caster\'s core magical Tiers are not static. They can be
enhanced through powerful magic, legendary artifacts, or profound
personal transformation. These enhancements are a form of \"cheating\"
the normal progression of experience, allowing a character to
temporarily or permanently touch a level of power beyond their natural
station. These boosts are governed by a strict set of rules and caps to
maintain cosmic balance.

A. The Two Types of Boosts

\* \*\*Temporary Boosts:\*\* These are fleeting increases in power,
granted by magic items (a \"+2 Staff of Fire Mastery\"), temporary
enhancement spells, or situational blessings. They represent a momentary
surge of power.

\* \*\*Permanent Boosts:\*\* These are lasting, fundamental changes to a
character\'s soul and magical potential. They are much harder to come
by, typically resulting from a life-altering event like a magical
mutation, a divine gift, or a transformation into a monstrous or epic
being.

B. The Rules of Stacking and Caps

To prevent runaway power, the system of magical attainment is governed
by a series of hard caps. Bonuses from different sources can stack, but
only up to these defined limits.

\* \*\*The Permanent Cap:\*\* A character can only benefit from a
maximum of \*\*+2\*\* in permanent boosts to their \*\*Overall Mastery
Tier\*\* and a maximum of \*\*+3\*\* to any single \*\*Effect\*\* or
\*\*Subtype Tier\*\*.

\* \*\*The Temporary Cap:\*\* Similarly, a character can only benefit
from a maximum of \*\*+2\*\* in temporary boosts to their \*\*Overall
Mastery Tier\*\* and \*\*+3\*\* to any single \*\*Effect\*\* or
\*\*Subtype Tier\*\*.

\* \*\*The Combined Cap:\*\* The total bonus from \*all\* sources
(temporary and permanent combined) cannot exceed \*\*+4\*\* for
\*\*Overall Mastery\*\* or \*\*+6\*\* for any single \*\*Effect/Subtype
Tier\*\*.

C. The Prodigy Exception

A character\'s innate nature can allow them to exceed these limits.

\* The \*\*Prodigy Aptitude\*\* is not a bonus; it is a fundamental
quality of the character\'s soul. Its benefit of allowing a caster to
exceed their \`Overall Mastery\` by +1 for a specific focus \*\*stacks
on top of\*\* all other boosts.

\* A character who is \*\*Inept\*\* (-1 to max ESL) in a field can use a
+1 temporary or permanent boost to achieve a \"net wash,\" effectively
canceling out their natural disadvantage.

D. Interacting with Power Tiers (Mortal, Epic, Deific)

This is the most crucial rule, governing the very nature of ascension.

\* \*\*Temporary Boosts Cannot Break Tiers:\*\* A temporary boost from
an item or spell can raise a character\'s Tier up to the maximum for
their current state of being, but no further. An Archmage with a natural
\*\*Overall Mastery of 9\*\* who dons a \"+2 Amulet of Mastery\" is
capped at \*\*Overall Mastery 10\*\* (the peak of the Mortal Tier). The
item cannot grant them true epic power.

\* \*\*Permanent Boosts CAN Break Tiers:\*\* A permanent boost is a
fundamental change to a character\'s very nature. This is how ascension
happens. An Archmage with a natural \*\*Overall Mastery of 10\*\* who
undergoes a horrifying transformation into an \*\*Eidolon\*\* might gain
a \*\*+1 permanent boost\*\* to their Overall Mastery. This doesn\'t
just raise their number; it \*\*pushes them into the Epic Tier\*\*,
making their new \*\*Overall Mastery 11\*\*. They have broken the mortal
limit by becoming something more than mortal.

Foreword: Magic is not a static list of incantations to be memorized; it
is a living language. The so-called \"spells\" taught in academies and
whispered in covens are merely common phrases and established
prose---recipes proven to be effective and stable. The true master,
however, does not merely recite poetry; they compose it. They understand
the fundamental grammar of reality and can shape it to their will. This
text is a primer on that grammar.

I. The Foundation of Magic: The Prima Æther

The source of all magical power in the world of Tessera is a pervasive,
fundamental force known as the Prima Æther. It is not a single, uniform
field, but a vast and complex magical \"atmosphere\" that operates with
its own turbulent weather patterns. This magical climate is the result
of the constant, dynamic interplay between two fundamentally different
and incompatible magical systems that were forcibly merged during the
Ancient Magical Calamity. These two systems are not good or evil; they
are simply different laws of reality, personified by the dual gods of
magic, Kanon and Anima.

A. The Primal Current: The World\'s Internal Wellspring (Anima\'s Law)

The Primal Current is the true, native magic of Tessera itself. It is
the world\'s own \"internal\" magic, a raw, chaotic, and life-giving
energy that is generated by the planet, its vibrant ecosystems, and its
unique magical materials. It is the domain of Anima, the Wellspring of
Chaos. This current is akin to an alternating current (AC); it is wild,
cyclical, and intuitive, ebbing and flowing like a tide. Practitioners
of Innate Magic (Sorcerers, Psions, Sōmaturges) are individuals who have
a deep, personal connection to this internal current of the world,
cultivating their own internal \"spark\" into a roaring fire.

B. The Structured Current: The Imposed Lattice (Kanon\'s Law)

Layered on top of the Primal Current is the Structured Current. It is
the domain of Kanon, the Architect of Order. This current is akin to a
direct current (DC); it is orderly, systematic, and predictable,
blanketing the world in a rigid \"lattice\" or \"grid\" of magical law.
Practitioners of Studied Magic (Wizards, Clerics) are specialists who
learn the complex rules necessary to safely tap into and manipulate this
overlying, artificial grid.

II\. The Lexicon of Power: The Spell Sentence

All magical expressions can be deconstructed into a foundational \"spell
sentence\". By combining these fundamental components, a caster gives
form and function to the raw Prima Æther.

The components of a spell sentence are as follows:

\* \*\*Effect:\*\* The \"verb\" of the spell. This defines the spell\'s
core action and what it fundamentally does.

\* \*\*Subtype:\*\* The \"adjective\" that qualifies the Effect. This
provides the spell with its elemental, conceptual, or energetic nature.

\* \*\*Target:\*\* The \"noun\" of the spell. This defines who or what
the spell affects.

\* \*\*Range:\*\* The \"preposition\" indicating distance.

\* \*\*Duration:\*\* The \"adverb\" of time, dictating how long the
Effect persists.

\* \*\*Additives (Augmentations):\*\* Enhancements that make a spell
more complex or powerful.

\* \*\*Subtractives (Anchors):\*\* Limitations or foci that make a spell
easier to control and less taxing to cast.

III\. The Tiers of Mastery: Power, Discipline, and Focus

A caster\'s ability is not a monolithic measure of \"power.\" True
mastery is nuanced and is understood through three distinct, independent
tiers of proficiency. A character\'s aptitude in these tiers is rated on
a scale from 1 to 20, divided into three categories of power: Mortal
(1-10), Epic (11-15), and Deific (16-20).

A. Overall Mastery

This is the measure of a caster\'s total potential---their raw power,
force of will, and connection to the magical energies of the world. It
governs the absolute ceiling of what they can accomplish, acting as a
hard cap for the other two tiers. It also determines the maximum amount
of Æther Points (ÆP) a caster can spend on a single spell.

B. Effect Tier

This represents a caster\'s deep understanding and discipline within a
particular magical \"verb\" or Effect (e.g., Damage, Affliction,
Create). One can achieve grandmastery in the Effect of Damage, capable
of weaving spells of pure destruction, yet remain a novice in the Effect
of Create. The Effect Tier determines a spell\'s raw power (its Base
Effect Rank) and provides a pool of \"Construction Points\" used to
define its Target, Range, and Duration.

C. Subtype Tier

This is the tier of fine control and deep specialization over a magical
\"adjective\" or Subtype (e.g., Fire, Cold, Mind). A high Subtype Tier
allows a caster to create vast, complex, and efficient spells of that
type by providing a \"Complexity Pool\" to purchase Additive words. A
caster\'s skill with a specific Subtype also determines their accuracy
when casting spells of that nature.

IV\. The Casting Process: The Interlocking Cap

A spell\'s final power is not determined by a single tier, but by the
interplay between them. The core principle is that a caster cannot apply
their skill with more precision than the power they are actually
channeling.

\* \*\*Effective Spell Level (ESL):\*\* The final \"power level\" of any
given spell is capped by the lesser of the relevant Effect Tier and
Subtype Tier. A caster with Damage (Effect Tier 7) and Fire (Subtype
Tier 5) can cast a fire damage spell with a maximum ESL of 5.

\* \*\*Accuracy Cap:\*\* The Attack Bonus for a spell is equal to the
caster\'s Subtype Tier, but it cannot exceed the ESL of the spell being
cast.

V. The Spell Recipe: Construction & Casting

A \"spell\" is a stable, repeatable formula, or \"recipe,\" that a
caster has researched and mastered. The process of creating and casting
these recipes is governed by the Tiers of Mastery.

**Mastery Capacity Pool Mechanics**

**Overview**

The Mastery Capacity Pool is a core mechanic that measures a caster\'s
ability to divide their focus and sustain multiple, resource-intensive
magical effects simultaneously.

\* Purpose: This pool is the resource consumed by any spell or effect
that uses the Tethered Additive to achieve a Continuous duration (such
as summoned creatures, ongoing buffs, or persistent wards).

\* Costs: The cost to maintain a Tethered effect is equal to the Mastery
Rating of that specific effect. This cost is \"reserved\" from the pool
for as long as the effect is active.

Calculating Your Pool

A character\'s pool is determined by their Overall Mastery Tier (OMT)
and their investment in the \"Expanded Consciousness\" talent.

\* Base Pool: Overall Mastery Tier x 4

\* Maximum Pool: Overall Mastery Tier x 34

\* Current Pool: (OMT x 4) + ((OMT x 3) \* \# of \"Expanded
Consciousness\" purchases)

\> Key Rule: Dynamic Scaling

\> The bonus from the Expanded Consciousness talent is dynamic. It is
not fixed at the level you purchased it. The bonus from all previous
purchases scales with your current Overall Mastery Tier.

\>

Expanding the Pool: \"Expanded Consciousness\" Talent

This talent allows a character to specialize as a controller, summoner,
or master of persistent magic by expanding their pool from the base
value toward the maximum.

\* Effect: Each time this talent is purchased, it adds (Overall Mastery
Tier x 3) to the character\'s Mastery Capacity Pool.

\* Purchase Limit: A character can purchase this talent a maximum number
of times equal to their current Overall Mastery Tier.

\* The Path to Mastery: It takes exactly 10 total purchases to reach the
absolute maximum pool. Due to the purchase limit, this can only be
achieved at Mastery Tier 10.

\* Talent Cost: The XP cost starts at 5 and increases linearly with each
purchase.

This example shows how a character\'s pool grows and scales as they gain
mastery.

A. The Unified Power Formula

A caster\'s ability to shape a spell, both in its application (Target,
Range, Duration) and its complexity (Additives), is determined by their
total magical aptitude. This is represented by a single, unified formula
that creates two distinct resource pools.

\* \*\*Unified Pool Formula:\*\* Pool Size = (1/2 Effect Tier) + (1/2
Subtype Tier) + (Overall Mastery Tier) + Integrity.

B. The Construction Pool (Application)

A spell\'s application---its Target, Range, and Duration---is determined
by a pool of Construction Points (CP) that are spent to \"buy\" these
qualities from a set menu.

\* \*\*Construction Pool Size:\*\* Calculated using the Unified Pool
Formula.

C. The Complexity Pool (Augmentation)

A spell\'s complexity---the number and power of its Additives---is
determined by a separate pool of Complexity Points. These
are a different form of construction point, essential for augmentation
but not for the spell's basic function.

\* \*\*Complexity Pool Size:\*\* Also calculated using the Unified Pool
Formula.

\* The total cost of all Additives in a spell\'s recipe cannot exceed
this budget. Subtractives can be taken to grant bonus points to this
pool, representing a trade-off of a limitation for greater complexity.

D. The Final Recipe (Cost and Power)

Once all components are chosen, the spell's final power level and
casting cost are determined.

\* \*\*Final Rank:\*\* The spell\'s total complexity is calculated.
Final Rank = (Base Effect Rank) + 
(Additive Costs) - (Subtractive
Savings).

\* \*\*Effective Spell Level (ESL):\*\* The Final Rank is then used to
determine the spell\'s ESL on a scale from 1 to 20.

\* \*\*Æther Point (ÆP) Cost:\*\* The ESL determines the base ÆP cost to
cast the spell, using the formula: Base ÆP Cost = (ESL × 2) -- 1.

VI\. The Principle of Equivalence: The Art of the Spell

It is a foundational law of thaumaturgy that there are myriad paths to a
single destination. The final result of a spell is often more important
than the method of its casting. While a novice learns rote formulae, a
master understands that a desired outcome can be achieved through
different, yet equally valid, spell constructions.

Consider the goal of neutralizing a foe:

\* The Fulminator of Stormpeak may achieve this with a single,
devastating blast of raw force---a \`Damage\` spell with an
\`Instantaneous\` Duration.

\* The Eschatologist of Mortis Ævum may achieve a similar result by
weaving a withering hex that saps vitality over time---a \`Weaken\`
spell with a \`Sustained\` Duration.

\* The Limenologist of the Sovereign Exchange may accomplish the same
goal by calling forth a lesser elemental to fight for them---a
\`Summon\` spell.

Though the methods are entirely different, the total power expended and
the ultimate result can be equivalent. This principle is the heart of
magical innovation and the very soul of the art. It proves that true
power lies not in the spells you know, but in your understanding of how
to build them.

VII\. Casting Philosophies: The Architect and the Improviser

In Tessera, every individual possesses a spark of magical potential. The
path to true power is not defined by rigid classes, but by the caster\'s
own psychology, innate proclivities, and the philosophical approach they
take to magic. While a master may learn techniques from multiple paths,
most practitioners have a natural \"handedness\" that draws them to one
of two primary philosophies: the meticulous science of the Architect or
the intuitive art of the Improviser.

A. The Studied Caster: The Architect of Magic

The Architect\'s power comes from meticulous study, perfect design, and
an unparalleled understanding of magical theory. Their magic is a
science, and their spell recipes are optimized blueprints. This is the
path most often walked by Wizards, academic Clerics, and Artificers.

\* \*\*Primary Advantage (Recipe Optimization):\*\* When an Architect
creates a spell recipe, their deep understanding allows them to make it
incredibly efficient. The recipe gains a Final Rank reduction that
scales with its power:

\* ESL 1-4 recipes receive a -4 Final Rank reduction.

\* ESL 5-7 recipes receive a -3 Final Rank reduction.

\* ESL 8-10 recipes receive a -2 Final Rank reduction.

\* \*\*Secondary Advantage (Complexity Mastery):\*\* Architects are
masters of complex magical structures. They receive a bonus to their 
Complexity Pool equal to their Dominion score divided by 2 (rounded
down).

\* \*\*Weakness (Tactical Rigidity):\*\* An Architect\'s spells are
perfectly crafted but static. They cast the recipe exactly as it was
written and cannot alter its components on the fly. To change a spell,
they must go back to the research phase and design a new, equally
optimized recipe.

B. The Innate Caster: The Improviser of Magic

The Improviser\'s power comes from their intuitive, personal connection
to the Prima Æther. Their magic is an art, and their spell recipes are
familiar melodies upon which they can freely improvise. This is the path
of Sorcerers, Psions, and Sōmaturges.

\* \*\*Primary Advantage (On-the-Fly Modification):\*\* When an
Improviser casts a spell, they can, as part of the action, freely swap
the Additives or even the Primary Subtype within that spell\'s recipe.
The new combination must still be valid and cannot exceed their
Complexity Pool.

\* \*\*Secondary Advantage (Metamagic Affinity):\*\* Improvisers are
naturally gifted at pushing their limits. The additional ÆP cost for
them to use Metamagic Talents is reduced by 2 (to a minimum of 1).

\* \*\*Weakness (Inefficient Design):\*\* An Improviser\'s spell recipes
are not as optimized as an Architect\'s. They do not benefit from any
Final Rank reduction. Their strength is their adaptability, but their
foundational spells are inherently less efficient.

C. The Path of the Polymath

True mastery of magic is rare and difficult, requiring an individual to
become adept in multiple disciplines. A character can learn both Studied
and Innate techniques, keeping separate repertoires of optimized recipes
and flexible improvisational forms. A warrior may be a student of a
studied Ætherforged Precept while also cultivating the innate power of a
Sōmaturge. These \"polymaths\" are the most versatile and often most
powerful figures in the world, as they have a deep understanding of the
full, dualistic nature of the Prima Æther.

with a specific Effect is measured by their Effect Tier, which

build using that Effect.

target\'s physical form or represent \"meta\" abilities that grant
broad, flexible capabilities beyond that of a standard spell. These are
often the signature arts of powerful, specialized casters.

\| Effect \| Base Cost \| Core Function & Description \|

\| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \|

\| \*\*Elongation\*\* \| 1 \| A minor physical alteration that allows
the target to stretch their body and limbs, extending their reach. \|

\| \*\*Extra Limbs\*\* \| 1 \| A specialized physical alteration that
grants the target additional manipulative limbs, such as arms or
prehensile tentacles. \|

\| \*\*Growth\*\* \| 2 \| A classic transmutative art that allows a
target to temporarily increase their size, granting them greater
strength and resilience. \|

\| \*\*Shrinking\*\* \| 2 \| The inverse of Growth, this Effect allows a

X. The Spell Sentence Components: The Subtypes (Part 1)

The Subtype is the \"adjective\" of a spell, the Word that qualifies and
gives texture to the \"verb\" of the Effect. A caster\'s skill with a
specific Subtype is measured by their Subtype Tier, which governs the
complexity of the spells they can create with that energy or concept.
The Subtypes are divided into two categories: the Primary Subtype, which
defines \*what\* the spell is, and the Secondary Subtype, which defines
\*how\* it is made.

A. Primary Subtypes (The Base)

The Primary Subtype is a spell\'s core nature, its energy type, or the
material it manipulates. This is the fundamental palette of magical
expression.

i\. Elemental & Energy Types

These represent raw, energetic forces.

\* \*\*Fire:\*\* The Subtype of combustion, heat, and light.

\* \*\*Cold:\*\* The Subtype of entropy, ice, and the absence of heat.

\* \*\*Electricity:\*\* The Subtype of lightning, conductivity, and raw
energy.

\* \*\*Acid:\*\* The Subtype of corrosion, dissolution, and chemical
decay.

\* \*\*Sonic:\*\* The Subtype of vibration, sound, and resonance.

\* \*\*Light:\*\* The Subtype of illumination, radiance, and perception.

\* \*\*Shadow/Darkness:\*\* The Subtype of obfuscation, darkness, and
negative energy.

\* \*\*Æther:\*\* The Subtype of raw, unfiltered Prima Æther. It
represents pure magical energy without a specific elemental or
conceptual \"flavor,\" often manifesting as blasts of pure telekinetic
force or shimmering shields.

ii\. Material & Physical Types

These represent the manipulation of physical matter.

\* \*\*Earth:\*\* The Subtype of stone, soil, and crystal.

\* \*\*Air:\*\* The Subtype of wind, weather, and gases.

\* \*\*Water:\*\* The Subtype of liquids and pressure.

\* \*\*Metal:\*\* The Subtype of all forms of metal, from common iron to
rare, magically-infused ores.

\* \*\*Plant:\*\* The Subtype governing all forms of flora.

\* \*\*Animal:\*\* The Subtype related to the manipulation or emulation
of mundane beasts.

\* \*\*Crystal:\*\* The Subtype governing crystalline structures.

iii\. Metaphysical & Conceptual Types

These represent the manipulation of more abstract or esoteric forces.

\* \*\*Mind:\*\* The Subtype of thought, consciousness, psychic force,
and direct emotional influence.

\* \*\*Life:\*\* The Subtype governing vitality, biological processes,
and positive energy.

\* \*\*Death/Undeath:\*\* The Subtype governing necrotic energy, the
cessation of life, and the animation of the dead.

\* \*\*Time:\*\* The Subtype of temporal manipulation.

\* \*\*Space:\*\* The Subtype of spatial manipulation and teleportation.

\* \*\*Poison:\*\* The Subtype of venom, toxins, and debilitating
biological agents.

\* \*\*Disease:\*\* The Subtype of sickness, plagues, and pathogenic
effects.

\* \*\*Artifice:\*\* The Subtype of constructs, clockwork, and magitech.

\* \*\*Fate:\*\* The Subtype of causality, probability, and luck.

\* \*\*Gravity:\*\* The Subtype of mass, density, and attraction.

\* \*\*Emotion:\*\* A more focused Subtype dealing with the raw, primal
forces of feeling---fear, rage, joy, despair.

\* \*\*Order/Law:\*\* The Subtype for spells that actively impose a set
of rules on reality.

\* \*\*Imagination:\*\* The Subtype used for creating and manipulating
mental constructs.

X. The Spell Sentence Components: The Subtypes

B. Secondary Subtypes (The Flavor/Technique)

The Secondary Subtype is the \"modular\" layer of a spell. It describes
the underlying magical philosophy or technique used to shape the spell,
often reflecting the college that teaches it, the divine concept it
embodies, or the cultural tradition from which it originates. It
represents the \"how\" and \"why\" of the magic, not just the \"what.\"

i\. The Divine Values

This is the largest category of Secondary Subtypes. It represents a
caster weaving the core philosophy of a divine aspect into their magic.
Spells with these Subtypes don\'t just produce an effect; they express a
worldview. This list represents the full spectrum of divine philosophies
a caster can align with.

\* \*\*Sovereignty / Pragmatism\*\* (From Aurelion)

\* \*\*Development / Preservation\*\* (From Moros)

\* \*\*Artifice / Innovation\*\* (From Daedalon)

\* \*\*Fairness / Security\*\* (From Nomos)

\* \*\*Strategy / Suffering\*\* (From Bellum Hegemon)

\* \*\*Opportunity / Predation\*\* (From Valorin)

\* \*\*Community / Systemic\*\* (From Agora)

\* \*\*Nurture / Primal\*\* (From Verdena)

\* \*\*Expression / Chaos\*\* (From Rhapsodia)

\* \*\*Academic / Intuitive\*\* (From Kanon & Anima)

\* \*\*Lore / Gnosis\*\* (From Vestus)

\* \*\*Potential / Agency\*\* (From The Selûnae - Kismet/Sabina)

\* \*\*Connection / Alliance\*\* (From The Selûnae - Rhiannon/Livia)

\* \*\*Causality / Revelation\*\* (From The Selûnae - Vespera/Thessa)

\* \*\*Passion\*\* (From Isolde)

\* \*\*Friendship\*\* (From Silas)

\* \*\*Family\*\* (From Aethel)

\* \*\*Selflessness\*\* (From Eselle)

\* \*\*Playfulness\*\* (From Corin)

\* \*\*Duty\*\* (From Constantine)

\* \*\*Self-Love\*\* (From Kenna)

\* \*\*Hospitality\*\* (From Zeniel)

\* \*\*Devotion\*\* (From Veyra)

ii\. Methodological, Cultural, & Heritage Descriptors

This category is for techniques not directly tied to a deity\'s
philosophy, but to a specific methodology, cultural style, or ancestral
origin.

\* \*\*Psionic:\*\* The effect is manifested through pure mental force,
often with subtle, non-flashy aesthetics.

\* \*\*Runic:\*\* The effect is channeled through carved or drawn
symbols of power, a specialty of Deepguard Academy.

\* \*\*Sōmaturgic:\*\* The effect is shaped from the caster\'s own life
force (Vitality), often appearing as a semi-tangible construct of energy
bound to their body.

\* \*\*Æther-forged:\*\* The effect is produced through a studied,
martial discipline, blending physical action with magical philosophy.

\* \*\*Katalysis:\*\* A rare and dangerous Subtype for powers derived
from a pact with a Lacuna. The magic often feels alien and paradoxical.

\* \*\*Sanguine:\*\* The effect is fueled by the power of blood, a
specific and often forbidden technique.

\* \*\*Planar:\*\* The effect is infused with the specific environmental
energies of another plane of existence (e.g., \`Planar (Verdant Font)\`,
\`Planar (Borderlands)\`).

\* \*\*Alchemical:\*\* The effect is not cast, but is delivered through
a crafted, consumable medium---a potion, a bomb, or an inhaled gas.

\* \*\*Heritage:\*\* The effect is a direct result of a creature\'s
ancestral nature (e.g., \`Heritage (Draconic)\`, \`Heritage (Fey)\`).

XI\. The Spell Sentence Components: The Modifiers

Modifiers are the final layer of the spell sentence, the \"adverbs\" and
\"clauses\" that alter the core function of the spell. They are divided
into two categories: Additives, which enhance a spell at a cost, and
Subtractives, which limit a spell for a benefit.

A. Additives (Augmentations)

Additives are enhancements that make a spell more powerful or versatile.
The total cost of all Additives in a spell\'s recipe is paid for from
the caster\'s Complexity Pool.

i\. Application & Targeting Additives

These modifiers change how a spell is delivered or who it can affect.

\| Additive \| Cost \| Description \|

\| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \|

\| \*\*Affects Objects\*\* \| +2 \| A \"shortcut\" that allows a spell
which normally only works on creatures to affect inanimate objects. \|

\| \*\*Chain\*\* \| +3 \| When an instantaneous, single-target spell

well. \|

\| \*\*Dimensional\*\* \| +2 \| Allows a spell to cross the immediate
planar boundary into an adjacent, coexistent plane (like the
Palimpsest), affecting targets there. \|

\| \*\*Indirect\*\* \| +1 \| A regional \"shortcut\" technique. The
spell\'s attack originates from a point other than the caster, allowing
it to bypass cover. \|

\| \*\*Linked\*\* \| +1 \| Fuses two or more different Effects into a
single spell recipe that activate simultaneously with one action. \|

\| \*\*Multiattack\*\* \| +3 \| Allows a spell to make multiple attacks

\|

\| \*\*Ricochet\*\* \| +1 \| A regional \"shortcut\" technique. A

\|

\| \*\*Split\*\* \| +1 \| Allows a single-target spell to be split

\| \*\*Selective\*\* \| +1 \| Allows the caster to choose a number of

ii\. Potency & Duration Additives

These modifiers increase a spell\'s raw power, resilience, or how long
it lasts.

\| Additive \| Cost \| Description \|

\| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \|

\| \*\*Persistent\*\* \| +3 \| The spell is exceptionally hard to

\|

\| \*\*Penetrating\*\* \| +2 / +4 / +6 \| A tiered Additive for

\| \*\*Secondary Effect\*\*\| +3 \| An instantaneous spell affects the

\|

\| \*\*Tenacious\*\* \| +2 \| The spell is harder to dispel and its

\| \*\*Tethered\*\* \| +2 \| Sets the spell\'s duration to

\| \*\*Released\*\* \| +4 \| Sets the spell\'s duration to

\| \*\*Suspended\*\* \| +3 \| A powerful \"stasis\" ability that allows

\| \*\*Toggleable\*\* \| +1 \| Modifies a \`Sustained\` or \`Tethered\`

These modifiers grant a spell unique tactical options or utility
functions.

\| Additive \| Cost \| Description \|

\| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \|

\| \*\*Alternate Effect\*\*\| +2 per \| Allows a single spell recipe to

\| \*\*Alternate Resistance\*\*\| +2 \| The spell targets a different

\| \*\*Coaxing\*\* \| +2 \| A subtle technique that allows a spell to

\| \*\*Conduit\*\* \| +1 \| Allows a caster to deliver a touch spell

\| \*\*Homing\*\* \| +2 \| If a projectile spell misses, it gets

XI\. The Spell Sentence Components: The Modifiers (Part 2)

B. Subtractives (Anchors)

Subtractives, also known as Anchors, are limitations, trade-offs, and
flaws woven into a spell\'s recipe. In return for accepting these
drawbacks, a caster gains bonus points to spend in their Construction
Pool or Complexity Pool, allowing for the creation of more powerful or
efficient spells. Each Subtractive provides a \"saving\" that reduces a
spell\'s final calculated Rank.

i\. Activation & Action Modifiers

These flaws alter the time and effort required to cast a spell.

\| Subtractive \| Saving \| Description \|

\| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \|

\| \*\*Activation\*\* \| -1 \| The spell requires a preparatory action
to ready before its main effect can be used with a subsequent action. \|

\| \*\*Concentration\*\* \| -2 \| A spell that would normally be \"fire
and forget\" now requires active concentration each round to maintain,
making the caster vulnerable to disruption. \|

\| \*\*Increased Action\*\*\| -1 to -4 \| A scalable flaw. A spell\'s
casting time is increased from a Standard Action to a Full-Round Action
(-1), one minute (-2), ten minutes (-3), or one hour or more (-4). \|

ii\. Limitation & Restriction Modifiers

These flaws limit the circumstances or targets for which a spell is
effective.

\| Subtractive \| Saving \| Description \|

\| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \|

\| \*\*Close Range Only\*\*\| -2 \| The spell\'s range is permanently
set to Touch and cannot be increased by Additives. \|

\| \*\*Limited\*\* \| -1 to -4 \| A scalable restriction. The spell only
works on a broad category of targets (-1), a specific sub-category (-2),
under a rare condition (-3), or under a unique, near-unrepeatable
condition (-4). \|

\| \*\*Removable\*\* \| -2 or -4 \| The spell\'s power is housed in an
item that is hard to remove (-2) or is easily disarmed or stolen (-4).
\|

\| \*\*Requires Vessel\*\*\| -1 \| The spell requires a physical medium
to inhabit or transform (e.g., a corpse for an undead, a pile of stone
for a golem). \|

\| \*\*Ritual Components\*\*\| -1 or -2 \| The spell requires either
verbal or somatic components (-1), or both (-2), making it vulnerable to
being silenced or bound. \|

\| \*\*Sense-Dependent\*\* \| -1 \| For the spell to work, the target
must perceive it with a specific sense (e.g., see a symbol, hear a note,
inhale a vapor). \|

\| \*\*Uncentered Area\*\* \| -1 \| When casting an area effect spell,
the caster must include their own position within the area of effect. \|

iii\. Risk & Consequence Modifiers

These flaws introduce a significant element of risk, danger, or personal
cost to casting a spell.

\| Subtractive \| Saving \| Description \|

\| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \| :\-\-- \|

\| \*\*Check Required\*\* \| -2 \| Casting the spell requires a
successful skill check. Failure wastes the action and any ÆP spent. \|

\| \*\*Contingent Control\*\*\| -2 \| Control of the spell\'s effect is

\| \*\*Delayed Activation\*\*\| -1 \| The spell cannot be used directly,

later. \|

\| \*\*Feedback\*\* \| -1 \| If the spell\'s manifestation is destroyed,
the caster suffers backlash damage. \|

\| \*\*Overwhelming\*\* \| -4 \| A Major Flaw. The spell becomes

\| \*\*Sacrifice\*\* \| -2 (Minor) or -4 (Major) \| The caster pays a

\| \*\*Side Effect\*\* \| -1 to -4 \| A scalable risk. If the spell

\| \*\*Uncontrolled Parameter\*\*\| -2 \| The caster can unleash the

\| \*\*Unstable\*\* \| -2 \| The spell\'s formula is volatile. Each
casting has a chance of mishap, causing it to fizzle, backfire, or
produce a thematically appropriate \"wild surge\" effect. \|

\| \*\*Unstable Property\*\*\| -2 \| The spell is cast with a beneficial

XII\. Magical Talents: The Art of Specialization

While the spell sentence provides the universal grammar of magic, a
caster\'s true genius is often expressed through the mastery of
specific, focused techniques that go beyond the basics. These are known
as Magical Talents---unique abilities learned through intense training,
divine insight, or as a manifestation of a unique heritage. They
represent a caster\'s hyper-focus in a particular area of their craft.

A. The Framework for Magical Talents

Talents are not spells, but enduring qualities of the caster. They are a
core part of a character\'s progression and are governed by a simple set
of rules.

\* \*\*Acquisition:\*\* Talents are purchased with experience points.
They represent a significant investment of time and training in a
specialized field.

\* \*\*Prerequisites:\*\* Many Talents have prerequisites, requiring a
minimum Overall Mastery Tier, knowledge of a specific Effect or Subtype,
a high alignment with a divine philosophy on the Psychē Schema, or the
prior mastery of another Talent.

\* \*\*Benefit:\*\* Each Talent provides a specific and unique
mechanical benefit, allowing a character to perform an action they
otherwise could not, or making them significantly better at an existing
one.

B. Metamagic Talents

These are active talents that allow a caster to augment their known
spell recipes on the fly, typically by spending additional Æther Points
(ÆP) at the time of casting. They are the tools of the tactical
improviser.

\* \*\*Empower Spell:\*\* By spending +2 additional ÆP, you can increase
all variable, numeric effects of a spell by 50%.

\* \*\*Maximize Spell:\*\* By spending +4 additional ÆP, you can treat
all variable, numeric effects of a spell as their maximum possible
value.

\* \*\*Quicken Spell:\*\* By spending a number of additional ÆP equal to
the spell\'s ESL × 2, you can cast a spell with a standard action
casting time as a swift/minor action.

\* \*\*Enlarge Spell:\*\* By spending +1 additional ÆP, you can double
the range of a spell.

\* \*\*Widen Spell:\*\* By spending +2 additional ÆP, you can double the
area of effect of a spell.

\* \*\*Extend Spell:\*\* By spending +1 additional ÆP, you can double
the duration of a spell.

\* \*\*Unbalanced Magic:\*\* You gain access to two maneuvers when
casting a spell with an attack roll:

\* \*Overwhelm:\* Take a penalty to your attack roll to add an equal
bonus to the spell\'s save DC or damage.

\* \*Pinpoint:\* Take a penalty to the spell\'s damage or save DC to add
an equal bonus to your attack roll.

C. Focus Talents

These are passive talents that represent a caster\'s deep affinity for a
specific type of magic, making them inherently more potent with it than
other casters.

\* \*\*Focus (Effect):\*\* Choose one specific \`Effect\` (e.g.,
\`Affliction\`). All spells you cast using this \`Effect\` are more
difficult to resist (e.g., the save DC increases by 1). This talent can
be taken multiple times to increase the bonus.

\* \*\*Focus (Subtype):\*\* Choose one specific \`Primary Subtype\`
(e.g., \`Fire\`). All spells you cast with this \`Subtype\` are more
difficult to resist.

\* \*\*Spell Penetration:\*\* You are adept at overcoming magical
defenses. You gain a bonus on any check made to overcome an opponent\'s
magical resistance or counter-magic effects like \`Nullify\`.

\* \*\*Warden Breaker:\*\* A specialized talent that grants a
significant bonus on checks to overcome powerful, static magical
defenses like a \`Nullify\` effect or a Deepguard ward.

\* \*\*Immunity Breach (Mastery Talent):\*\* \*Prerequisite: High
Overall Mastery.\* By spending a significant amount of additional ÆP,
you can temporarily suppress a creature\'s \`Immunity\` to a specific
Subtype for one round.

III. Character Creation Integration
1. Universal Grammar (The Freebies)
Every character, regardless of Intelligence or origin, inherently knows all 0 CP Components (Ray, Burst, Close Range, Instant/Concentration).

2. Regional Dialects (The GP Spend)
A character's "Magical Fluency" determines which advanced shapes they know at creation.
 * Fluency Resource: Grammar Points (GP) = Intellect + Dominion (or Anima)
 * Spending: Use GP to unlock components from the Common Curriculum (1-4 CP).
 * Example: A Smart Wizard (5 GP) might unlock Cone (1), Spread (2), and Medium Range (1).

2. Treasure Components (The Restricted List)

Components costing 5 CP or higher (Thick Walls, Unlimited Range, Permanence) cannot be selected via GP at creation. These must be learned as rewards (Treasure) or by spending a precious Talent Slot if the GM permits esoteric backgrounds.

IV. Balance Audit (The "Level 2" Check)
 * Test Case: Can a Mastery Level 3 (Pool: 7 CP) character cast a standard PF1e Level 2 spell like Web?
 * Web Cost: Spread (2) + Medium Range (1) + Minutes (1) = 4 CP.
 * Result: YES. (7 CP Pool > 4 CP Cost).
 * Design Note: The cost structure allows Novices (ML 3-4) to comfortably cast "Level 2 equivalent" spells, but requires them to reach Adept (ML 5) or specialize heavily to cast complex geometries like Fortified Walls or Unlimited Range.

## 1. Total ÆP Pool (Philosophy‑Agnostic)

**Goal:** Define a single Æther Point (ÆP) pool formula that does not care whether a caster is using Studied (Architect) or Innate (Improviser) methods, but that still scales with both innate potential and player investment in magical talents.

### ÆP Pool Formula

- Let **Dominion** be the character’s Metaphysical attribute governing active control of esoteric energies.
- Let **Mastery** be the character’s Overall Mastery rating.
- Let **Tₘ** be the **total number of Magical/Supernatural Talents** the character has purchased (including Metamagic Talents and any talents explicitly tagged as magical or supernatural).

Then:

> **Total ÆP Pool**
> $$
> \text{ÆP}_{\text{max}} = (\text{Dominion} + Tₘ) \times \text{Mastery}
> $$

Notes:

- **ÆP is agnostic to casting philosophy.** A character who knows both Architect and Improviser techniques uses the same ÆP pool for all spells.
- Increasing **Dominion** or buying more **magical/supernatural talents** both increase total ÆP linearly. There is **no hard cap** on Tₘ; as long as growth is additive, progression remains linear.
- This formula does not introduce new stats; it uses only **Dominion, Mastery, and existing Talents.**

## 2. Studied Magic (Architect): Intellect‑Based Metamagic Efficiency

**Goal:** Make Metamagic more efficient for Studied casters, keyed to **Intellect**, without gating access or making Metamagic free.

### Architect Metamagic Efficiency

This applies **only** when:

- The spell is cast **using a Studied/Architect recipe** (i.e., a pre‑constructed, optimized spell), and
- The caster applies one or more **Metamagic Talents** to that spell.

Procedure:

1. Calculate the spell’s **base ÆP cost** from its ESL as normal:
   $$
   \text{Base ÆP} = (ESL \times 2) - 1
   $$

2. Calculate the **total Metamagic surcharge** from all Metamagic Talents applied.

3. Let:
   $$
   M_{\text{int}} = \left\lfloor \dfrac{\text{Intellect}}{2} \right\rfloor
   $$

4. For **each individual Metamagic Talent**, reduce its extra ÆP cost by up to **M\_int**, but never below **+1 ÆP**:

   - New surcharge per talent:
     $$
     \text{Surcharge}_{\text{final}} = \max(1,\ \text{Surcharge}_{\text{base}} - M_{\text{int}})
     $$

5. The **final cost** of the spell is:
   $$
   \text{Final ÆP} = \text{Base ÆP} + \sum \text{Surcharge}_{\text{final (per metamagic)}}
   $$

Properties:

- High‑Intellect Architects get **strong Metamagic discounts**.
- No Metamagic surcharge ever drops below **+1 ÆP**, preventing “free” Metamagic.
- **Access** to Metamagic remains purely **Talent‑based**; Intellect only improves **efficiency**.

## 3. Innate Magic (Improviser): Anima‑Based Per‑Spell ÆP Reduction

**Goal:** Represent that Innate magic “flows more easily” through the soul, by tying **per‑spell cost reduction** to **Anima**, while explicitly preventing any form of ÆP recovery via casting.

### Innate ÆP Cost Reduction

This applies **only** when:

- The spell is being cast via **Innate/Improviser** expression (improvised or flexible form), regardless of whether that recipe also exists in a Studied form.

Procedure:

1. Determine the spell’s **ESL** and compute its **Base ÆP** from ESL:
   $$
   \text{Base ÆP} = (ESL \times 2) - 1
   $$

2. Compute the **Anima Discount**:
   $$
   \text{Anima Discount} = \max(0,\ \text{Anima} - 3)
   $$

3. Apply the discount, but **floor at 0**:
   $$
   \text{Innate Base Cost} = \max(0,\ \text{Base ÆP} - \text{Anima Discount})
   $$

4. Add any applicable **Metamagic surcharges** on top of this:

   - Innate casters pay the **full base surcharge** for each Metamagic Talent (they no longer receive an across‑the‑board Metamagic cost reduction).
   - Each Metamagic Talent always adds **at least +1 ÆP** (by the global Metamagic rule).

5. Final cost:
   $$
   \text{Final ÆP} = \text{Innate Base Cost} + \sum \text{Metamagic Surcharges}
   $$

Explicit rule:

- If intermediate math would drive cost **below 0**, the spell’s ÆP cost is treated as **0**, not negative.
- **Casting a spell can never restore or generate ÆP.** A “0‑cost” spell merely costs **no additional ÆP**; it does not refund or recover ÆP.

Properties:

- High‑Anima Innate casters can cast many **low‑ESL, unmodified spells at 0 ÆP**, matching the “magic comes easily” fantasy.
- Adding Metamagic to an Innate spell **always** costs ÆP (minimum +1 per Metamagic), so spells can never be used as an ÆP battery.
- The reduction is tied directly to **Anima** and uses only your existing ESL→ÆP formula.

## 4. Updated Philosophy Separation Summary

Append this as a clarification to the “Architect and Improviser” section.

### Studied Caster – The Architect of Magic

- **Core Resource:**
  - Uses the universal ÆP pool:
    $$
    \text{ÆP}_{\text{max}} = (\text{Dominion} + Tₘ) \times \text{Mastery}
    $$
- **Existing Advantages:**
  - **Recipe Optimization:** FR reduction by ESL band (‑4 / ‑3 / ‑2) on Architect recipes.
  - **Complexity Mastery:** Bonus to Complexity Pool equal to **⌊Dominion ÷ 2⌋**.
- **New Advantage:**
  - **Intellect‑Based Metamagic Efficiency:**  
    Metamagic surcharges on Architect recipes are reduced by **⌊Intellect ÷ 2⌋** each, to a minimum of **+1 ÆP per Metamagic**.
- **Weakness:**
  - **Tactical Rigidity:** Recipes are fixed; changing effects requires redesign.

### Innate Caster – The Improviser of Magic

- **Core Resource:**
  - Uses the **same universal ÆP pool** as everyone else:
    $$
    \text{ÆP}_{\text{max}} = (\text{Dominion} + Tₘ) \times \text{Mastery}
    $$
- **Existing Advantages:**
  - **On‑the‑Fly Modification:** May freely swap Additives or Primary Subtype within a known recipe at cast time, within Complexity limits.
- **New Advantage (replacing global Metamagic discount):**
  - **Anima‑Based ÆP Reduction:**  
    For Innate castings, per‑spell Base ÆP is reduced by **(Anima − 3)** (minimum 0), floored at **0 ÆP**, with Metamagic surcharges added afterward.
- **Removed/Changed:**
  - The previous flat “Metamagic Affinity: −2 ÆP to Metamagic cost” as a general Innate advantage is **replaced** by the Anima‑based per‑spell cost reduction.

- **Weakness:**
  - Innate recipes **do not** gain FR reduction; their foundation remains less structurally efficient than Architect designs, especially at higher ESL and with heavy Metamagic.
