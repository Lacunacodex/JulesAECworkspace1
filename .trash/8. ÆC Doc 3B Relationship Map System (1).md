**RELATIONSHIP MAP SYSTEM**

The Topology of Connection and Consequence

Document 3B --- Vertebra 3: Fronts (Companion)

*How fronts connect to each other and how consequences propagate through
the network*

**WHAT THE RELATIONSHIP MAP IS**

The relationship map is the wiring diagram for consequence propagation.
Every front exists in a network of connections to other fronts. When a
clock advances on one front, the advancement produces pressure that
travels along the connections to other fronts. Which fronts feel the
pressure, how much pressure arrives, and what form it takes are all
determined by the properties of the connections between them.

Without the map, fronts are isolated countdowns. Each clock ticks toward
its own terminal state in a vacuum. With the map, fronts are a system.
Each clock's advancement affects other clocks through the network of
connections. The emergent complexity of the setting comes from these
interactions --- outcomes that no single front would produce on its own
but that the network produces through propagation.

The map is simultaneously a prep tool (the GM draws it to understand the
setting's power structure), a play tool (the GM reads it to determine
how consequences cascade), and a diagnostic tool (the GM examines it to
find where the pressure is accumulating and which connections are
approaching state changes).

**NODES**

Every entity that can drive a front or be affected by one is a node on
the map. Nodes are not limited to NPCs. Any entity with a Schema or
Schema-equivalent (Document 1B) can be a node.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **NPC Nodes**      Individual characters with personal Schemas. Each
                     NPC's Assessment Card (Document 2B) feeds the node's
                     data. The NPC's front potential section on the card
                     lists which fronts this node drives.

  **Institutional    Colleges, guilds, governments, military
  Nodes**            organizations. Each institution has an institutional
                     Schema (Document 1B) and drives institutional
                     fronts. Institutional nodes are larger than NPC
                     nodes --- they contain multiple NPCs within them,
                     each of whom may also be an individual node.

  **Location Nodes** Reactive environments that function as fronts
                     (Project Slaughterhouse). The Titanwood, the Crash
                     Site, the Pythia Gauntlet. Location nodes connect to
                     institutional and NPC nodes through operational
                     relationships.

  **Territorial      Regions defined by Zoetrope position and isotope
  Nodes**            profile. Territorial nodes contain institutional,
                     NPC, and location nodes within them. They operate at
                     the longest timescale and connect to other
                     territorial nodes through trade, diplomacy, and
                     conflict.

  **The Party**      The party is a node. It has connections to NPCs,
                     institutions, and locations. Its edges track the
                     same three properties (trust, debt, tension) as
                     every other connection. The party's position on the
                     map reveals which parts of the network they can
                     influence and which are beyond their current reach.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

**Node Hierarchy**

Nodes nest. A territorial node contains institutional nodes. An
institutional node contains NPC nodes. The map can be drawn at different
zoom levels depending on what the GM needs to see:

**Adventure zoom:** Shows only the party, the NPCs they've met, and the
connections between them. The most common view during play. Fits on one
page.

**Campaign zoom:** Shows institutions, their inter-institutional
connections, and the key NPCs who drive each institution's fronts. Used
during prep to understand the political landscape.

**Setting zoom:** Shows territories, their inter-territorial
connections, and the institutional forces operating within each. Used
for long-arc planning and Zoetrope tracking.

The same map at three resolutions. The edges between nodes carry the
same three properties at every zoom level. Trust between two NPCs and
trust between two nations describe the same thing at different scales.

**EDGES**

Every connection between two nodes is an edge with three properties. The
properties are rated on a qualitative scale (Low / Medium / High) rather
than numerical values. The qualitative rating is sufficient for the GM
to make propagation decisions during play.

**Trust**

How freely information flows between the two nodes. Trust governs the
quality of communication --- not whether nodes communicate (most do),
but how honestly and completely.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **High Trust**     Honest, complete communication. Each node shares
                     relevant information willingly. Intelligence flows
                     freely in both directions. Information passed along
                     this edge is reliable --- the receiving node can act
                     on it without verification.

  **Medium Trust**   Filtered communication. Each node shares some
                     information but withholds what they consider
                     sensitive. Intelligence is partial. Information
                     passed along this edge should be verified before
                     critical decisions.

  **Low Trust**      Guarded or deceptive communication. Each node
                     assumes the other is withholding or distorting.
                     Intelligence is unreliable. Information passed along
                     this edge may be deliberately misleading --- the
                     receiving node must treat it as unverified at best,
                     disinformation at worst.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

Trust determines how QUICKLY and ACCURATELY consequences propagate. When
a clock advances on a front, the symptoms travel to connected fronts
along edges. High-trust edges transmit accurate symptoms rapidly --- the
connected front knows what happened and responds appropriately.
Low-trust edges transmit distorted symptoms slowly --- the connected
front learns something happened but misunderstands what, and responds
based on the misunderstanding.

Distorted propagation along low-trust edges IS a story generator. Front
A's clock advances. Front B learns about it through a low-trust edge and
receives a garbled version. Front B responds to the garbled version,
producing consequences that make no sense to Front A. The
miscommunication cascades. Neither front understands why the other is
behaving strangely. The party, encountering both fronts' symptoms, sees
two sets of anomalous behavior and must investigate to discover that the
source is a miscommunication, not a conspiracy.

**Debt**

Latent obligations between two nodes. Who owes whom, and what. Debt is
the stored energy in the relationship --- it creates leverage and shapes
behavior even when not actively invoked.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Owes**           This node owes the connected node. The connected
                     node has leverage --- the ability to call in the
                     debt at a moment of their choosing. The indebted
                     node's behavior is partially constrained by the
                     awareness that the debt could be called.

  **Owed**           The connected node owes this one. This node has
                     leverage. The debt is an asset that can be deployed
                     strategically --- called in during a crisis, traded
                     to a third party, or held as insurance.

  **Mutual**         Both nodes owe each other. Neither has clear
                     leverage. The debts may be of different kinds (one
                     owes money, the other owes loyalty) creating a
                     complex interdependency that neither can cleanly
                     resolve.

  **None**           No outstanding obligations. The cleanest possible
                     relationship state. Also the least interesting ---
                     no stored energy means no leverage and no
                     constraint.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

Debt interacts with trust. A high-trust edge with debt is a genuine
obligation that both parties acknowledge --- the debt will be honored
because the relationship supports it. A low-trust edge with debt is a
weapon --- the debt will be called at the worst possible moment because
the debtor does not trust the creditor to be reasonable. Debt on a
low-trust edge is a bomb waiting for a trigger.

Unresolved debt accumulates tension (see below). Every session that
passes with an outstanding debt increases the tension on the edge by one
step. The longer a debt goes uncalled, the more weight it carries when
it finally arrives. This is Chekhov's Gun applied to relationships ---
every placed debt must eventually resolve, and the delay between
placement and resolution increases the dramatic impact.

**Tension**

Current stress on the connection. The pressure that accumulates from
unresolved conflicts, competing interests, and outstanding debts.
Tension is the measure of how close the relationship is to a state
change.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Low Tension**    The relationship is stable. No active conflicts. No
                     pressing unresolved issues. The edge is quiet.
                     Consequences propagate along it without producing
                     secondary effects.

  **Medium Tension** Active stress. Competing interests, unresolved
                     disagreements, or accumulated small frictions. The
                     edge is loaded. Consequences propagating along it
                     may amplify --- a small clock advancement on one
                     front could trigger a disproportionate response from
                     the connected front because the tension converts
                     neutral information into threatening information.

  **High Tension**   The relationship is approaching a state change. One
                     more significant event will push it past the
                     threshold. The edge is a fault line. Any consequence
                     propagating along it will be amplified dramatically
                     --- even minor events become crises because the
                     relationship has no remaining capacity to absorb
                     stress.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

**Threshold Crossings**

When tension on an edge reaches High and a triggering event occurs, the
state changes one step in the direction of the tension's source:

**Negative tension:** Tension from competing interests or unresolved
conflict pushes the state toward hostility: Ally → Neutral → Rival →
Enemy.

**Positive tension:** Tension from an act of sacrifice, trust-building,
or debt resolution pushes the state toward cooperation: Enemy → Rival →
Neutral → Ally.

After a state change, tension resets to Low. The relationship has
reorganized into its new state. Tension begins accumulating again from
the new baseline.

State changes are dramatic events. They should emerge from play, not be
imposed arbitrarily. The GM tracks tension qualitatively through
accumulated events. When a specific in-game event pushes the
relationship past the threshold, the state change happens in response to
that event, producing a visible moment the party (and the affected NPCs)
can react to.

**State Changes as Bangs**

Every state change is a bang at Vertebra 4 (Clocks) --- a situation that
demands an immediate response with no predetermined right answer. When
two allied institutions become rivals, every NPC connected to both must
choose a side, renegotiate their position, or find a way to maintain
both relationships. The state change produces forced-choice situations
that cascade through the network. The more edges connected to the
changed relationship, the more NPCs and fronts are affected, and the
more bangs are generated.

**CONSEQUENCE PROPAGATION**

When a clock advances on a front, the consequences travel along the
edges to connected fronts. The propagation follows four rules.

**Rule 1: Consequences Follow Edges**

Consequences do not jump between unconnected nodes. If Front A's clock
advances and Front A has no edge to Front C, then Front C does not feel
the consequences directly. Front C may feel them indirectly if Front B
is connected to both A and C --- the consequences propagate from A to B,
then from B to C. Each intermediary edge filters and potentially
distorts the consequence.

**Rule 2: Trust Governs Accuracy**

A consequence propagating along a high-trust edge arrives accurately.
The receiving front knows what happened and can respond appropriately. A
consequence propagating along a low-trust edge arrives distorted. The
receiving front may misunderstand the cause, the severity, or the intent
behind the event. The distortion IS a story seed --- the
misunderstanding produces responses that create new complications.

**Rule 3: Tension Governs Amplification**

A consequence propagating along a low-tension edge arrives at its actual
magnitude. A consequence propagating along a high-tension edge is
AMPLIFIED. The receiving front interprets the event as more threatening,
more significant, or more urgent than it actually is because the loaded
relationship converts neutral information into alarming information. A
routine clock advancement on one front, propagating through a
high-tension edge, can trigger a crisis-level response on the connected
front --- not because the event warranted it but because the
relationship's stored stress amplified it.

**Rule 4: State Governs Direction**

The edge's state determines WHETHER the consequence helps or hinders the
receiving front. An ally edge converts consequences into support. An
enemy edge converts consequences into threat. A neutral edge converts
consequences into data. A rival edge converts consequences into
competitive pressure. The same clock advancement produces four different
effects depending on which state the connecting edge occupies.

These four rules combined produce emergent complexity. A single clock
advancement propagates through multiple edges, each with different
trust, tension, and state values, producing different effects at each
receiving front. The network of edges transforms one event into multiple
consequences, each filtered and shaped by the connection it travels
through. The GM does not need to author each consequence individually.
The map's properties determine them.

**BUILDING AND MAINTAINING THE MAP**

**Initial Construction**

Draw the map during prep, after the fronts are defined (Document 3A).
Place every active front as a node. Draw an edge between every pair of
fronts that have a relationship --- institutional, personal, geographic,
or economic. Assign the three properties (trust, debt, tension) and the
state (ally/neutral/rival/enemy) to each edge based on the setting's
established situation.

Not every pair of fronts needs an edge. Only fronts that interact,
compete, cooperate, or share resources need connections. A Stormpeak
front and a Sovereign Exchange front operating in the same territory are
connected. A Stormpeak front in the far north and a Sovereign Exchange
front on the southern coast probably are not, unless a specific
relationship links them.

**Adding Nodes During Play**

When a new NPC becomes notable (triggers an Assessment Card) or a new
front emerges from play, add them to the map. Draw edges to existing
nodes based on the relationships that have been established. New nodes
start with few edges. Over the campaign, they accumulate connections as
relationships form.

The party is a node. As they form relationships with NPCs, make
agreements with institutions, and establish presence in territories,
their edges accumulate. The party's map of connections IS their
political influence. A party with many ally-state edges to powerful
nodes has significant structural leverage. A party with few edges is
isolated and vulnerable to being acted upon without allies.

**Updating Properties**

After each session, review the map for changes:

**Trust changes:** Did any event increase or decrease trust between two
nodes? Successful cooperation increases trust. Betrayal or deception
decreases it. Adjust accordingly.

**Debt changes:** Were any debts created, called in, or traded? New
favors, unmet obligations, or transfers of leverage change the debt
landscape.

**Tension changes:** Did any event increase tension between nodes?
Competing over the same resource, conflicting clock objectives, or
propagated consequences from other edges all increase tension.
Resolution of conflicts or acts of reconciliation decrease tension.

**State changes:** Did any edge's tension cross the threshold? If yes,
process the state change and reset tension to Low.

**Physical Format**

The map is whatever the GM can maintain. Practical options:

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **Index cards on a One card per node (doubling as the Assessment Card).
  wall**             String or drawn lines between cards for edges.
                     Properties written on the strings or noted on the
                     cards. Maximum visibility. Requires wall space.

  **Digital graph    Any diagramming tool that supports labeled edges.
  tool**             Scalable, searchable, shareable. Requires screen and
                     software comfort.

  **Notebook         A hand-drawn diagram in the campaign notebook.
  sketch**           Compact and portable. Harder to update cleanly. Best
                     for small networks (under 15 nodes).

  **Spreadsheet      Nodes on rows and columns. Each cell contains the
  matrix**           edge properties for that pair. Compact, easy to
                     update, but loses the spatial intuition of a visual
                     map.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

The format does not matter. What matters is that the GM can look at the
map and immediately answer: "When Front A's clock advances, which other
fronts feel it, through what quality of connection, and how will they
respond?" If the format answers that question at a glance, it is
sufficient.

**THE MAP AND THE PARTY**

The party sees the NPC Assessment Cards. They do not see the map. The
map is a GM tool. But the party BUILDS the map through play without
knowing it.

**Player-Visible Outputs**

When the party encounters two NPCs who react to each other, they are
seeing an edge. When an NPC's behavior changes because of something that
happened to a different NPC three sessions ago, the party is seeing
consequence propagation along an edge they may not have mapped. When an
NPC who was helpful becomes hostile after the party allied with their
rival, the party is seeing a state change triggered by their own actions
modifying the network.

The party's investigative instinct --- "why did the merchant suddenly
become hostile?" --- IS the party detecting a state change and tracing
the edge that caused it. The investigation leads to the rival
relationship they disrupted. The map becomes visible to the party
through its EFFECTS, not through its structure.

**The Party as Network Actor**

Every party action that affects an NPC's relationship modifies an edge.
Helping an NPC increases trust and potentially shifts the state toward
ally. Betraying an NPC increases tension and shifts toward rival or
enemy. Making a promise creates debt. Fulfilling a promise resolves
debt. The party is not outside the network --- they are a node within
it, subject to the same propagation rules as every other node.

This means the party's actions have NETWORK consequences, not just
individual ones. Allying with the Wardens is not just a relationship
with one institution --- it modifies the Wardens' allies' trust in the
party (positive propagation), and the Wardens' rivals' tension with the
party (negative propagation). The party's alliance choice cascades
through the network the same way any front's clock advancement does.

Fulcrum Point spends at Distance 3 (reaching into Fronts from
Adventures) are the party directly manipulating an edge. Spending FP to
alter a relationship's trust, debt, or tension is the player reaching
into the map and changing its wiring. The deferred consequences of that
spend (spine self-correction through skipped vertebrae) are the network
rebalancing around the altered edge.

**MAP PATTERNS**

Certain configurations of nodes and edges produce recognizable story
structures. The GM can read the map for these patterns to identify where
the most dramatic potential lives.

  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------
  **The Triangle**   Three nodes where A is allied with both B and C, but
                     B and C are rivals. A is forced to choose, mediate,
                     or manage competing loyalties. The party in a
                     triangle is under maximum social pressure. Classic
                     dramatic geometry.

  **The Bridge**     Two clusters of nodes connected by a single edge.
                     The bridge node or edge is a chokepoint --- if it
                     breaks, the two clusters are isolated from each
                     other. The party occupying the bridge position has
                     enormous structural leverage but is also the most
                     vulnerable to being cut.

  **The Web**        One central node connected to many peripheral nodes.
                     The central node is a hub --- consequences propagate
                     through it to the entire network. If the hub is
                     disrupted, all connected nodes lose their primary
                     communication channel. Headmasters and institutional
                     leaders are often hubs.

  **The Chain**      Nodes connected in a line: A→B→C→D. Consequences
                     propagate sequentially with distortion at each link.
                     By the time an event travels from A to D, it may be
                     unrecognizable. This is the representation chain
                     expressed as network topology.

  **The Island**     A node with no edges or only one edge. Isolated from
                     the network. Cannot receive propagated consequences
                     and cannot influence the network. An island NPC is
                     narratively inert unless the party creates
                     connections to them. An island NPC who SHOULD be
                     connected but isn't is a red flag --- someone
                     severed the edges deliberately.

  **The Pressure     Multiple nodes all connected to each other with high
  Cooker**           tension on every edge. Any event triggers cascading
                     state changes across the entire cluster. Maximum
                     dramatic potential. The GM should place the party's
                     most important adventure inside or adjacent to a
                     pressure cooker.
  ------------------ ----------------------------------------------------

**BETWEEN-SESSION MAP MAINTENANCE**

A quick checklist for updating the map after each session.

  ------- ------------------------------------------------------------------
  **☐**   **Action**

  ☐       Add any new nodes (new notable NPCs, newly discovered fronts,
          newly entered territories).

  ☐       Draw edges from new nodes to existing nodes where relationships
          were established this session.

  ☐       Review every edge the party interacted with. Did trust, debt, or
          tension change?

  ☐       Check for faction turn consequences propagating from fronts that
          advanced between sessions (see Document 3C).

  ☐       Check every high-tension edge. Did any triggering event push it
          past the threshold? Process state changes.

  ☐       Check for debt aging. Every session an outstanding debt goes
          uncalled, tension on that edge increases one step.

  ☐       Identify the current pressure cookers. Where is the tension
          highest? That is where the next session's most dramatic potential
          lives.

  ☐       Update the party's node. What edges did they create, strengthen,
          weaken, or sever this session?
  ------- ------------------------------------------------------------------

This checklist takes five to ten minutes after each session. It is the
minimum maintenance that keeps the map accurate and the consequence
propagation functional. Skipping it for one session is recoverable.
Skipping it for three creates a map that no longer reflects the
setting's actual state, and the GM loses the ability to predict how
consequences cascade.
